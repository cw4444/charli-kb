# Source Index

This bibliography is organized around the path from Bell inequalities to observer-dependent facts and local friendliness. Each entry records the source type, claim, assumptions, relevance, and caveats.

## Core Bell Background

### Bell 1964 - "On the Einstein Podolsky Rosen Paradox"

- Authors: John S. Bell
- Year: 1964
- Source type: peer-reviewed paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysicsPhysiqueFizika.1.195
- Summary: Derives an inequality that any local hidden-variable theory must satisfy, then shows quantum mechanics predicts violations for entangled systems.
- Core claim: No theory satisfying Bell's locality condition and the relevant hidden-variable assumptions can reproduce all quantum predictions.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Supplies the mathematical template later reused in Wigner's-friend variants: if a set of intuitive assumptions holds, certain correlations must obey an inequality.
- Assumptions used/challenged: locality/local causality, hidden variables or pre-existing outcomes, measurement-setting independence.
- Tags: Bell theorem, nonlocality, hidden variables, locality
- Confidence / caveats: high; foundational result with many later refinements.
- Mode: theoretical

### CHSH 1969 - "Proposed Experiment to Test Local Hidden-Variable Theories"

- Authors: John F. Clauser, Michael A. Horne, Abner Shimony, Richard A. Holt
- Year: 1969
- Source type: peer-reviewed paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.23.880
- Summary: Gives the experimentally convenient CHSH inequality for testing Bell-type constraints.
- Core claim: Local hidden-variable theories obey a bound that quantum systems can violate.
- Observer-dependent relevance: The CHSH structure is the direct ancestor of Bell-type inequalities in observer-independence and local-friendliness scenarios.
- Assumptions used/challenged: locality, realism or jointly defined outcomes, free choice.
- Tags: CHSH, Bell inequality, experiment design
- Confidence / caveats: high.
- Mode: theoretical / experimental proposal

### Aspect, Dalibard, Roger 1982 - "Experimental Test of Bell's Inequalities Using Time-Varying Analyzers"

- Authors: Alain Aspect, Jean Dalibard, Gerard Roger
- Year: 1982
- Source type: peer-reviewed experimental paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.49.1804
- Summary: Landmark photon experiment using changing analyzer settings; results matched quantum predictions and violated Bell inequalities.
- Core claim: Quantum correlations violate Bell-type constraints under experimental conditions designed to address locality concerns.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Establishes Bell violation as an empirical phenomenon, not just a mathematical curiosity.
- Assumptions used/challenged: local realism; remaining loopholes later addressed by subsequent tests.
- Tags: Bell test, experiment, entanglement
- Confidence / caveats: high; historically important, not fully loophole-free by modern standards.
- Mode: experimental

### SEP 2019 - "Bell's Theorem"

- Authors: Wayne Myrvold, Marco Genovese, Abner Shimony
- Year: 2019 revision
- Source type: encyclopedia / scholarly overview
- Link: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2019/entries/bell-theorem/
- Summary: Careful philosophical and technical overview of Bell's theorem, assumptions, and interpretations.
- Core claim: Bell's theorem is a family of results deriving constraints from local causality plus auxiliary assumptions.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Helps avoid oversimplified slogans such as "Bell proves nonlocality" without specifying assumptions.
- Assumptions used/challenged: local causality, probability, measurement independence, hidden variables.
- Tags: Bell theorem, philosophy of physics, assumptions
- Confidence / caveats: high as secondary synthesis.
- Mode: philosophical / interpretive

## Wigner's Friend And Extended Wigner's Friend

### Wigner 1961 - "Remarks on the Mind-Body Question"

- Authors: Eugene P. Wigner
- Year: 1961
- Source type: book chapter / philosophical physics essay
- Link: https://philpapers.org/rec/WIGROT
- Summary: Introduces the Wigner's friend thought experiment: a friend observes a quantum system inside a sealed lab while Wigner treats the whole lab quantum mechanically.
- Core claim: Applying quantum mechanics to observers exposes tension around measurement, consciousness, and collapse.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Original source of the observer-vs-superobserver setup.
- Assumptions used/challenged: collapse, observer role, universal applicability of quantum mechanics.
- Tags: Wigner's friend, measurement problem, consciousness
- Confidence / caveats: high as historical source; Wigner's consciousness emphasis is not required in modern versions.
- Mode: theoretical / philosophical

### Frauchiger and Renner 2018 - "Quantum Theory Cannot Consistently Describe the Use of Itself"

- Authors: Daniela Frauchiger, Renato Renner
- Year: 2018
- Source type: peer-reviewed theoretical paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-05739-8
- Summary: Uses an extended Wigner's-friend thought experiment with agents reasoning about one another to argue that assumptions Q, C, and S cannot all hold.
- Core claim: If quantum theory is universal, agents' predictions are mutually consistent, and outcomes are single, the scenario yields contradiction.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Makes cross-observer consistency itself a target assumption.
- Assumptions used/challenged: Q universal quantum validity, C consistency between agents, S single-world outcomes.
- Tags: Frauchiger-Renner, extended Wigner's friend, self-reference
- Confidence / caveats: high that the theorem follows from its formal assumptions; controversial whether those assumptions are jointly required by reasonable interpretations.
- Mode: theoretical

### Lazarovici and Hubert 2019 - "How Quantum Mechanics Can Consistently Describe the Use of Itself"

- Authors: Dustin Lazarovici, Mario Hubert
- Year: 2019
- Source type: peer-reviewed theoretical critique
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-37535-1
- Summary: Argues that the Frauchiger-Renner contradiction disappears when the physical situation is described completely, especially in Bohmian mechanics.
- Core claim: Single-world quantum theories need not be inconsistent if one avoids mixing incompatible descriptions.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Important counterweight to strong claims that extended Wigner's-friend arguments refute all single-world views.
- Assumptions used/challenged: challenges cross-context reasoning and incomplete descriptions.
- Tags: critique, Frauchiger-Renner, Bohmian mechanics
- Confidence / caveats: medium-high; interpretation-dependent.
- Mode: theoretical / interpretive

### Steane 2025 - "The Extended Wigner's Friend, Many-and Single-Worlds and Reasoning from Observation"

- Authors: Andrew Steane
- Year: 2025
- Source type: peer-reviewed theoretical / interpretive paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10701-025-00831-8
- Summary: Reassesses extended Wigner's-friend reasoning, isolation assumptions, quantum erasure of observations, and the single-world/many-worlds divide.
- Core claim: The arguments do not cleanly distinguish single-world from many-world interpretations; reasoning from observations must account for possible erasure.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Recent limitation on broad claims from Wigner-friend no-go arguments.
- Assumptions used/challenged: isolation, subjective collapse, reasoning from erased records.
- Tags: critique, extended Wigner's friend, many worlds, single world
- Confidence / caveats: medium-high; recent interpretive analysis.
- Mode: theoretical / interpretive

## Observer-Independent Facts And Local Observer-Independence

### Brukner 2018 - "A No-Go Theorem for Observer-Independent Facts"

- Authors: Časlav Brukner
- Year: 2018
- Source type: peer-reviewed theoretical paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/e20050350
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.00749
- Summary: Derives a Bell-like no-go theorem for the joint objectivity of facts recorded by friends and superobservers in an extended Wigner's-friend setup.
- Core claim: Universal quantum theory, locality, freedom of choice, and observer-independent facts cannot all be true in the scenario.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Central source for the claim that facts may be relational rather than absolute.
- Assumptions used/challenged: universal quantum validity, locality, free choice, observer-independent facts.
- Tags: Brukner, observer-independent facts, Wigner's friend, Bell-type inequality
- Confidence / caveats: high for theorem under assumptions; interpretation of "facts" remains disputed.
- Mode: theoretical

### Proietti et al. 2019 - "Experimental Test of Local Observer Independence"

- Authors: Massimiliano Proietti, Alexander Pickston, Francesco Graffitti, Peter Barrow, Dmytro Kundys, Cyril Branciard, Martin Ringbauer, Alessandro Fedrizzi
- Year: 2019
- Source type: peer-reviewed experimental paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aaw9832
- Summary: Six-photon experiment implementing a Wigner's-friend-style Bell inequality and reporting a violation by about five standard deviations.
- Core claim: Under locality and free-choice assumptions, the result supports an observer-dependent reading of quantum facts.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Main experimental anchor for the observer-independent-facts discussion.
- Assumptions used/challenged: locality, free choice, observer-independent facts, modelling photons as friend/record systems.
- Tags: Proietti, experiment, local observer independence, six photons
- Confidence / caveats: high for the measured inequality violation; medium for broad "objective reality does not exist" headlines. The experiment uses photonic proxies, not human-scale observers, and the observer-dependent conclusion depends on the stated assumptions.
- Mode: experimental

### Brukner 2022 - "Wigner's Friend and Relational Objectivity"

- Authors: Časlav Brukner
- Year: 2022
- Source type: Nature Reviews Physics commentary
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s42254-022-00505-8
- Summary: Compact review connecting Wigner's friend, Brukner's theorem, Proietti, local friendliness, QBism, and relational quantum mechanics.
- Core claim: Recent results motivate treating objectivity of measurement outcomes as relative to observation and observer.
- Observer-dependent relevance: A clear synthesis by the central theorist.
- Assumptions used/challenged: observer-independent outcomes, relational objectivity.
- Tags: relational objectivity, review, Brukner
- Confidence / caveats: high as authorial synthesis; not neutral between interpretations.
- Mode: interpretive / review

## Local Friendliness

### Bong et al. 2020 - "A Strong No-Go Theorem on the Wigner's Friend Paradox"

- Authors: Kok-Wei Bong, Aníbal Utreras-Alarcón, Farzad Ghafari, Yeong-Cherng Liang, Nora Tischler, Eric G. Cavalcanti, Geoff J. Pryde, Howard M. Wiseman, and collaborators
- Year: 2020
- Source type: peer-reviewed theoretical and experimental paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-020-0990-x
- Summary: Formulates local friendliness using assumptions weaker than Bell local causality and shows quantum violations in an extended Wigner's-friend setting.
- Core claim: No theory satisfying local agency and absoluteness of observed events can reproduce all quantum predictions.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Recasts the issue as "absoluteness of observed events" rather than hidden variables.
- Assumptions used/challenged: local agency, no superdeterminism, absoluteness of observed events.
- Tags: local friendliness, absoluteness, Wigner's friend, Bell
- Confidence / caveats: high for formal result; physical implementation still depends on idealized controllable observers/friends.
- Mode: theoretical / experimental

### Haddara and Cavalcanti 2025 - "Local Friendliness Polytopes in Multipartite Scenarios"

- Authors: Marwan Haddara, Eric G. Cavalcanti
- Year: 2025
- Source type: peer-reviewed theoretical paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.111.012206
- Summary: Studies the geometry of local friendliness correlations in multipartite extended Wigner's-friend scenarios.
- Core claim: Local friendliness defines a polytope of allowed correlations; quantum predictions can violate its facets.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Extends the technical toolkit beyond the original two-lab scenario.
- Assumptions used/challenged: local friendliness assumptions in multipartite cases.
- Tags: local friendliness, polytopes, multipartite
- Confidence / caveats: medium-high; technical extension.
- Mode: theoretical

### Walleghem et al. 2025 - "Connecting Extended Wigner's Friend Arguments and Noncontextuality"

- Authors: Laurens Walleghem, Yìlè Yīng, Rafael Wagner, David Schmid
- Year: 2025
- Source type: peer-reviewed theoretical paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2025-07-31-1819
- Summary: Relates local friendliness scenarios to Kochen-Specker noncontextuality and shows ways to derive local-friendliness inequalities from contextuality tools.
- Core claim: Extended Wigner's-friend arguments and noncontextuality are deeply connected but not identical.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Clarifies whether the novelty of local friendliness is really about observers or about contextual value assignment.
- Assumptions used/challenged: noncontextuality, absoluteness, compatibility of measurements.
- Tags: noncontextuality, local friendliness, Kochen-Specker
- Confidence / caveats: medium-high; recent technical result.
- Mode: theoretical

## Critiques And Limitations

### Okon 2025 - "Reassessing the Strength of a Class of Wigner's Friend No-Go Theorems"

- Authors: Elias Okon
- Year: 2025
- Source type: peer-reviewed critical paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/e27060563
- Summary: Critiques Brukner's observer-independent-facts theorem and the local-friendliness theorem, arguing they have shortcomings that limit their force.
- Core claim: These theorems may fail to impose significant new constraints on physical reality beyond known contextuality or Bell-style limits.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Main recent skeptical reassessment; essential for avoiding one-sided coverage.
- Assumptions used/challenged: observer-independent facts, local friendliness, novelty and validity of no-go claims.
- Tags: critique, reassessment, Wigner's friend, local friendliness
- Confidence / caveats: medium-high; philosophical/technical critique that itself may be contested.
- Mode: theoretical / philosophical

### Kastner 2021 - "The 'Delayed-Choice' Quantum Eraser, Retrocausality, and Wigner's Friend"

- Authors: Ruth E. Kastner
- Year: 2021
- Source type: arXiv / later interpretive critique
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01773
- Summary: Argues that some Wigner's-friend/local-observer-independence claims misattribute outcomes where, on her interpretation, no such outcomes obtain.
- Core claim: Apparent irreconcilable facts can arise from applying outcome language outside its valid context.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Pushes back against the conclusion that observers literally disagree on facts.
- Assumptions used/challenged: outcome attribution, collapse/transactional interpretation framing.
- Tags: critique, transactional interpretation, Proietti, Wigner's friend
- Confidence / caveats: medium; interpretation-specific.
- Mode: interpretive / philosophical

## Interpretations And Adjacent Views

### Fuchs and Schack 2013 - "Quantum-Bayesian Coherence"

- Authors: Christopher A. Fuchs, Rüdiger Schack
- Year: 2013
- Source type: peer-reviewed review paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.85.1693
- Summary: Presents QBism, where quantum states and probabilities are personalist degrees of belief used by agents.
- Core claim: The Born rule constrains an agent's expectations rather than describing an observer-independent wavefunction.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Offers a natural home for agent-relative quantum facts.
- Assumptions used/challenged: objective quantum state, impersonal probabilities.
- Tags: QBism, agents, probability, interpretation
- Confidence / caveats: high as QBist source; interpretive stance remains disputed.
- Mode: interpretive / philosophical

### Fuchs, Mermin, and Schack 2014 - "An Introduction to QBism with an Application to the Locality of Quantum Mechanics"

- Authors: Christopher A. Fuchs, N. David Mermin, Rüdiger Schack
- Year: 2014
- Source type: peer-reviewed pedagogical paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4874855
- Summary: Introduces QBism and applies it to locality, presenting quantum states and probabilities as personalist agent expectations rather than objective physical states.
- Core claim: QBism treats measurement as an action by an agent on the world and treats outcomes as personal experiences for that agent.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Useful bridge between QBism, Wigner's friend, and the claim that quantum states are not observer-independent facts.
- Assumptions used/challenged: objective wavefunction, impersonal probability, nonlocal collapse.
- Tags: QBism, Mermin, agents, locality
- Confidence / caveats: high as QBist source; interpretive stance remains disputed.
- Mode: interpretive / pedagogical

### Adlam 2018 - "Spooky Action at a (Temporal) Distance"

- Authors: Emily Adlam
- Year: 2018
- Source type: peer-reviewed paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/e20010041
- Summary: Argues that quantum foundations should take temporal nonlocality seriously rather than assuming all physical explanation must be temporally local.
- Core claim: Temporal locality is a substantive assumption, not an obvious scientific requirement.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Adds a time/lawhood route for questioning naive local classical realism without making the observer central in the QBist sense.
- Assumptions used/challenged: temporal locality, standard forward-causal explanation.
- Tags: Emily Adlam, temporal nonlocality, retrocausality, quantum foundations
- Confidence / caveats: medium-high; source is directly relevant but not yet deeply summarized in a standalone source note.
- Mode: theoretical / philosophical

### Adlam 2018 - "Quantum Mechanics and Global Determinism"

- Authors: Emily Christine Adlam
- Year: 2018
- Source type: peer-reviewed paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.12743/quanta.v7i1.76
- Summary: Proposes that some quantum features may be perspectival effects arising because locally accessible variables reveal only part of an underlying deterministic global correlation structure.
- Core claim: No-signaling and quantum correlations may be explained from constraints on locally accessible perspectives within a larger global structure.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Suggests a route where local observer perspectives are incomplete views of a global pattern, distinct from QBist agent-relative quantum states.
- Assumptions used/challenged: local access as complete access, standard causal explanation of correlations.
- Tags: Emily Adlam, global determinism, perspectival effects, no-signaling
- Confidence / caveats: medium; needs deeper reading before strong claims.
- Mode: theoretical / interpretive

### Adlam 2022 - "Laws of Nature as Constraints"

- Authors: Emily Adlam
- Year: 2022
- Source type: peer-reviewed paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10701-022-00546-0
- Summary: Develops a realist account of lawhood where laws are constraints or modal structure, able to accommodate nonlocal, atemporal, all-at-once, retrocausal, or otherwise non-time-evolution laws.
- Core claim: Laws need not be understood only as dynamical rules evolving earlier states into later states.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Provides the best local source for the "all-at-once" / global-constraint side of the comparison with QBism.
- Assumptions used/challenged: time-evolution picture of laws, temporal locality, standard non-Humean lawhood.
- Tags: Emily Adlam, laws as constraints, all-at-once, block universe, retrocausality
- Confidence / caveats: medium-high from metadata and abstract; full paper should be ingested before detailed claims.
- Mode: philosophical / foundations

- Authors: Carlo Rovelli
- Year: 1996
- Source type: peer-reviewed theoretical / interpretive paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02302261
- Summary: Proposes that states and values are relative to physical systems rather than absolute.
- Core claim: Quantum mechanics describes relations between systems, not observer-independent intrinsic states.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Directly aligned with relational facts.
- Assumptions used/challenged: absolute state/value assignments.
- Tags: relational quantum mechanics, observer-relative facts
- Confidence / caveats: high as foundational RQM source; details of inter-observer consistency remain debated.
- Mode: interpretive / philosophical

### Everett 1957 - "'Relative State' Formulation of Quantum Mechanics"

- Authors: Hugh Everett III
- Year: 1957
- Source type: peer-reviewed theoretical paper
- Link / DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.29.454
- Summary: Introduces the relative-state formulation: no collapse; the universal wavefunction evolves unitarily.
- Core claim: Measurement correlates observer and system into branches rather than selecting one absolute outcome.
- Observer-dependent relevance: Evades single-outcome contradictions by rejecting a unique global outcome.
- Assumptions used/challenged: collapse and single-outcome uniqueness.
- Tags: Everett, many-worlds, relative state
- Confidence / caveats: high as historical source; probability and basis questions remain debated.
- Mode: theoretical / interpretive
