# Daniel Kahneman

Daniel Kahneman was a psychologist whose work with Amos Tversky helped build the modern study of judgment under uncertainty and behavioral economics.

For this wiki, Kahneman is useful as a boundary figure: he shows why intelligent people can make systematic judgment errors without being foolish, dishonest, or mystical. The relevant mechanism is not stupidity. It is salience, framing, availability, loss response, and confidence outrunning evidence.

## Useful Contributions

- Availability: what comes easily to mind can feel more frequent or probable.
- Heuristics and biases: human judgment uses shortcuts that are often efficient but predictably error-prone.
- Prospect theory: people evaluate gains and losses relative to reference points, with losses often carrying more force than equivalent gains.
- Focusing illusion: the factor currently in focus can be overestimated as a driver of overall well-being or life satisfaction.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Kahneman is not a general-purpose authority for every claim about the mind.
- Heuristics are not proof that humans are irrational trash fires. They are useful shortcuts with known failure modes.
- The focusing illusion is not a command to dismiss what matters. It is a warning to widen the frame before trusting felt importance.
