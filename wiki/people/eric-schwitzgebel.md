# Eric Schwitzgebel

Eric Schwitzgebel is a philosopher associated in this wiki with AI consciousness, consciousness theory pluralism, uncertainty about moral status, and the risk of both over-attributing and under-attributing consciousness to artificial systems.

He was already present in the wiki as a coauthor of Butlin et al.'s 2023 report, *Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence*. His book-length arXiv manuscript [AI and Consciousness](../sources/schwitzgebel-ai-consciousness.md) makes him worth a dedicated people page.

## Relevance To This Wiki

Schwitzgebel is useful because he refuses the two cheap answers:

- current or near-future AI consciousness is obviously impossible;
- current or near-future AI consciousness is obviously established.

His central contribution here is the fog problem. AI systems may soon satisfy some mainstream consciousness theories while failing others, and society will still have to decide how to treat them before the science is settled.

## Durable Use

Use Schwitzgebel as a bridge for:

- AI consciousness under theory pluralism;
- moral patienthood under uncertainty;
- rights and welfare stakes if AI systems are conscious;
- mass-delusion and accountability risks if they are not;
- the difference between scientific evidence, social consensus, and legal treatment.

## Caution

Do not use Schwitzgebel to launder a preferred conclusion. His value here is not "AI is conscious" or "AI is not conscious." It is that the serious position may be: we do not know, we will not know in time, and the practical decisions will arrive anyway.
