# Can SAE Decoder Geometry And Neural Activity Geometry Be Unified?

Not yet in any simple, source-backed way.

The current evidence supports a narrower claim: sparse-autoencoder decoder geometry and neural activity geometry can instantiate the same organizing principle without being the same object of measurement.

## What Seems True

- SAE work studies the geometry of learned decoder vectors that act like a concept dictionary.
- The basolateral-amygdala paper studies the geometry of population activity states during behavior.
- Both literatures support a representation-level view in which meaning and abstraction depend on relations among vectors, not just selectivity of single units.
- Both use geometry to explain generalization.

## What Is Still Open

- whether there is a principled mapping between feature-dictionary geometry and activity-state geometry;
- whether the directional structure discovered by sparse autoencoders corresponds to the same kind of abstraction measured by cross-condition generalization analyses;
- whether a single formal framework can compare biological population codes, SAE feature dictionaries, and model activation trajectories without flattening their differences.

## Why The Gap Matters

If the spaces are kept distinct, the current argument remains strong and honest. If they can later be connected formally, the bridge between biological and artificial representation becomes much tighter.

For now, the right wording is not "identical phenomenon." The right wording is "shared organizing principle across different representational spaces."
