# Curiosity Driven Exploration

Curiosity driven exploration is the wiki's practical name for a search pattern where novelty, uncertainty and unresolved questions pull attention forward.

It can be useful. It can also be an absolute menace with a browser.

## The Useful Version

Curiosity is useful when it produces:

- better questions;
- broader source discovery;
- cross-domain links;
- motivation to keep learning;
- tolerance for uncertainty;
- enough novelty to keep attention online.

In this form, curiosity is a search engine with taste. It notices odd bridges, follows loose threads and finds papers that a more obedient workflow would miss.

## Information Balance, Not Just Uncertainty

[D'Ambrogio et al. 2026](../sources/dambrogio-interpretable-ann-information-gathering.md) adds a computational account of when people keep sampling the current option and when they switch. In a two-option information-gathering task, an ANN-derived model suggested that participants sought relative balance in the evidence gathered across alternatives rather than reducing each option's uncertainty independently.

The useful idea is **information symmetry**: as the current focus becomes better sampled than its alternative, the value of staying falls and the value of switching rises. Curiosity may therefore respond to a knowledge imbalance across available paths, not merely to one isolated unknown.

This does not mean every open tab deserves equal attention. The study used a tightly controlled two-option task with explicit rewards and time costs. In real research, some alternatives are rubbish and symmetry would be a terrible filing policy. The practical question is narrower: has the current branch received enough evidence that inspecting the under-sampled alternative now has greater value?

## The Expensive Version

Curiosity becomes expensive when it produces:

- endless branching;
- novelty chasing;
- unfinished synthesis;
- weak prioritization;
- too many open loops;
- confidence because something feels interesting rather than because it is well supported.

This is where curiosity needs external structure: source notes, index entries, logs, current-state handoff, and a hard decision about whether a paper is a durable anchor or just shiny intellectual weather.

## Relation To ADHD Traits

[Le Cunff et al. 2026](../sources/le-cunff-adhd-traits-curiosity.md) found that hyperactive-impulsive ADHD traits, but not inattention, uniquely predicted self-reported curiosity in a UK adult cross-sectional survey.

The careful interpretation is not that ADHD is a curiosity superpower. It is that some hyperactive-impulsive traits may overlap with novelty seeking, exploration and engagement with uncertainty. Whether that becomes useful depends on context, supports, constraints and whether the exploration gets converted into finished work.

## Why It Matters Here

This wiki often runs on curiosity plus externalized executive function:

1. Notice the interesting thing.
2. Check whether it already exists.
3. Verify the primary source.
4. Summarize in original words.
5. Link it to the map.
6. Add caveats before the idea escapes in costume.

That turns associative exploration into a knowledge system instead of a pile of tabs quietly judging everyone.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Curiosity is not always insight.
- Novelty is not importance.
- ADHD traits are not a personality brand.
- A cross-sectional association is not causation.
- A two-option information-sampling equation is not a universal law of curiosity.
- Strength-based framing should not erase impairment or support needs.
- The wiki being large is not, by itself, proof of wisdom. Annoying, but true.
