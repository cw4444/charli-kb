# Fictional Finalism

Fictional finalism is an Adlerian idea: people are not guided only by past causes, but also by imagined future goals that organize present behavior.

The word "fictional" does not mean "worthless lie." It means the guiding goal is imagined, idealized, partly unverifiable, or not yet real. A person may still live as if that goal is true or necessary, and that as-if orientation can shape perception, emotion, attention, effort, avoidance, and action.

That makes fictional finalism useful for this wiki's optimism and agency lane. It gives a historical name for something the newer research keeps circling:

- imagined futures can organize present behavior;
- future expectancies can change effort and persistence;
- a self-image can act like a control policy;
- a useful fiction can guide action before it is literally true;
- a harmful fiction can trap a person inside impossible private rules.

## Adlerian Shape

In Adlerian psychology, the person is treated as a whole, goal-directed organism embedded in social life. The NCBI/StatPearls Adlerian therapy summary describes fictional finalism as the idea that human conduct is guided by a subjective final goal or guiding self-idea. The Adler Graduate School overview similarly frames the fictional final goal as a child-formed answer to inferiority that seems to promise future security and success.

The important move is teleological: ask not only "where did this come from?" but also "what future is this behavior serving?"

That does not erase the past. It changes the explanatory balance. Past experience matters because it helps form the guiding fiction, but the fiction then pulls present behavior toward an imagined endpoint.

## Healthy And Unhealthy Fictions

A guiding fiction can be useful when it is flexible, reality-contacting, and socially constructive:

- "I am becoming someone who can handle this kind of work."
- "My future self is a person who asks for help early."
- "This setback is information, not a final verdict."
- "I can move toward competence without pretending I am already perfect."

A guiding fiction becomes dangerous when it is rigid, private, grandiose, or detached from correction:

- "I must never fail."
- "I am only safe if I am admired."
- "If I am not exceptional, I am nothing."
- "The world must reward me because I imagined the outcome."

So the useful bridge is not "invent any future and believe hard enough." That is manifestation sludge with a hat on. The better version is: choose or revise a guiding fiction so it supports agency, preparation, contribution, and evidence-sensitive action.

## Relation To Optimism

Fictional finalism sits behind several optimism findings already in this wiki.

Best Possible Self imagery asks people to construct and rehearse a desirable future self. That is basically a short, structured, evidence-aware guiding-fiction exercise: not a guarantee, but a way to make a possible future more vivid and action-guiding.

Lisa Bortolotti's agency test also fits here. The question is not merely whether an optimistic belief is literally true right now. The question is whether it sustains competent action while preserving enough contact with evidence to update.

Placebo sleep is adjacent too. The participant's belief about sleep quality becomes part of the performance context. The story the system receives about itself can become one input into what the system does next.

## Relation To Ben Hardy / Future Self Work

Charli's comparison to Ben Hardy's future-self framing is useful, but keep the hierarchy clean.

The modern self-help version often says some sensible things: future identity can guide present choices; people behave differently when they vividly imagine who they are becoming; identity can be practiced through repeated choices.

Adlerian fictional finalism is the older, more structurally useful version for this wiki because it includes the danger as well as the promise. A future self can guide agency, but it can also become a tyrant, an escape hatch, or a private fiction that refuses correction.

## Relation To Future Self-Continuity

[Future Self-Continuity](future-self-continuity.md) supplies the missing decision-science mechanism beneath this bridge. Fictional finalism asks what imagined future goal organizes behaviour; future self-continuity asks whether the person living in that future feels connected, concrete, and valuable enough to influence a choice now.

The distinction matters. A desired future can be vivid but feel like it belongs to a glamorous stranger. A guiding fiction becomes more practically useful when present-you can see a credible line of identity and action leading to the person who receives the delayed benefit.

## Relation To Manifestation Language

[Manifestation As Behavior Modification](manifestation-as-behavior-modification.md) is the translation layer. When manifestation writing says "live in the state of the wish fulfilled," the useful reading is not cosmic ordering. It is: rehearse the identity, attention, choices, and environment that would make the desired future more reachable.

That fits fictional finalism when the guiding future changes behaviour. It fails when the person treats the imagined future as proof that evidence, constraints, other people, money, health, or time no longer matter. The wish fulfilled is useful as a control image, not as a forged receipt from reality.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Do not treat Adlerian psychology as fresh neuroscience.
- Do not say fictional finalism proves manifestation, reality creation, or mind-over-matter.
- Do not treat manifestation language as entirely useless; translate the useful parts into cognition, behaviour, attention, and agency.
- Do not use "fiction" to mean "fake, therefore useless."
- Do not use "future goal" to erase trauma, material constraint, social structure, illness, or poverty.
- Do not treat all pessimism as maladaptive; some future-negative simulation supports preparation.
- Do not treat all optimism as adaptive; some guiding fictions become invulnerability fantasy.
