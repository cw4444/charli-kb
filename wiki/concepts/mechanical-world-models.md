# Mechanical World Models

A mechanical world-model is a physical artifact that encodes a theory, approximation, or usable pattern of reality so a person can manipulate it and get predictions back.

The Antikythera mechanism is the anchor example. It was not intelligent in the modern AI sense. It did not learn, reason, or understand. But it turned astronomical cycles into a hand-powered interface: rotate the input, read the dials, and the machine gives structured answers about calendars, Moon phase, eclipse possibilities, and probably planetary positions.

That makes it useful for this wiki because it shows computation before electronics as a reality-facing act: humans build models that make the world queryable.

## Antikythera As Anchor

The mechanism matters because it compressed several layers of ancient knowledge into one object:

- observed sky cycles;
- mathematical astronomy;
- gear ratios;
- inscriptions and user-facing labels;
- civic calendars and games cycles;
- craft knowledge in bronze, wood, bearings, pins, and dials.

The lunar-motion detail is especially important. The Moon's apparent speed varies. Modern astronomy explains this through orbital dynamics, including elliptical orbit. The mechanism's makers did not need the modern explanation to approximate the phenomenon. They could encode the observed irregularity through cyclic theory and gearing.

That is the cool part: they could get a useful answer about a real pattern without having the final modern story about why the pattern happens.

## Why This Matters

Mechanical world-models separate three things that humans often blur together:

- **prediction:** the model gives useful outputs;
- **ontology:** the model's story about what reality is made of;
- **interface:** the form in which a human can query the model.

The Antikythera mechanism was predictive and interface-rich, but not ontologically modern. That makes it a good caution against a simple equation: good predictions do not automatically mean a model sees reality as it truly is.

This is the same brake that [Feynman, Calculation, and Reality Stories](../../themes/feynman-calculation-and-reality-stories.md) provides in the quantum lane. Calculation can work before interpretation settles down.

## AI Parallel

The AI comparison should stay narrow.

Modern AI systems also turn large bodies of pattern into queryable interfaces. A language model is not a bronze gear train, and the Antikythera mechanism is not an AI. But both belong to a broad history of artifacts that mediate between humans and complex reality:

- astronomical cycles become dials;
- workplace traces become a [Queryable Organization](queryable-organization.md);
- raw personal or research material becomes a wiki an agent can search;
- text, code, and records become model-accessible context.

The similarity is not agency. It is compression plus queryability.

Michael Levin's unconventional-cognition framing sharpens the AI side of this. In a 2026 public transcript, he argues that theories of computation describe the front-end interface of algorithms but may not be complete theories of every pattern or competency that comes through a system. His strongest AI-relevant point is that new AI systems may be exploring regions of cognition-space that have not previously been embodied.

For this wiki, the useful synthesis is:

- "just gears" is not a full account of the Antikythera mechanism;
- "just chemistry" is not a full account of a mind;
- "just linear algebra" may not be a full account of an AI system's behavioral competencies.

That does not mean current AI is conscious. It means substrate descriptions and functional ontology should not be confused. Predictive compression can be real before ontology catches up.

## Cultural Reference: Devs

Alex Garland's *Devs* is useful here as fiction about a prediction machine pushed to metaphysical extremes. Forest wants a deterministic system that can recover the past and forecast the future from complete state information. Lyndon's many-worlds move makes the machine work better but breaks Forest's single-world emotional demand.

The contrast is useful:

- Antikythera shows prediction before correct ontology.
- *Devs* imagines prediction as complete ontology.
- AI gives us prediction powerful enough to disturb ontology.

Forest's argument that total state information would allow total reconstruction is the extreme version of the same pattern. The wiki should not endorse hard determinism from this, but it should keep the deeper question: when a model becomes powerful enough, people start treating prediction as access to reality itself.

## Lost Lineages

The mechanism also matters because it disrupts a neat progress story. An artifact of this complexity implies a background of tools, workshops, mathematical practice, and predecessor devices. Most of that lineage did not survive.

That should make this wiki cautious about historical absence. Lack of surviving artifacts is not proof that the knowledge culture never existed. It is only proof that the archive is incomplete.

The same caution applies to AI and knowledge systems: future readers may inherit outputs without the tooling, context, prompts, failures, and social practices that produced them.

## Relevance To Reality

Mechanical world-models sit between reality and representation.

They are not passive descriptions. They are instruments that make some patterns easier to see, manipulate, and trust. When they work, they change what counts as obvious. A sky that once required specialist observation can become a dial. A messy archive can become a question-answering system. A private thought can become an artifact in minutes.

That is why this concept belongs near [Cognitive Latency Shock](cognitive-latency-shock.md): both involve reducing the action-distance between a question and an answer.

It also belongs near [Practical Agency Inside Constraint](practical-agency-inside-constraint.md). A mechanical world-model does not free a person from reality. It gives them a better handle on which relations, cycles, inputs, or boundary conditions matter.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Do not call every model a computer in the same sense.
- Do not call the Antikythera mechanism AI.
- Do not imply accurate prediction requires correct underlying ontology.
- Do not imply wrong ontology makes prediction useless.
- Do not cite Michael Levin as proof that AI is conscious.
- Do not use *Devs* as evidence for real quantum computing or hard determinism.
- Do not treat missing historical evidence as permission for fantasy.
- Do not collapse mechanical, symbolic, statistical, and agentic systems into one category.
