# Positive Alignment

Positive alignment is a proposed AI-alignment agenda that keeps ordinary safety constraints but adds a constructive target: AI should actively support human and ecological flourishing rather than merely avoid obvious harms.

The key contrast is:

- **Negative alignment:** prevent bad outcomes, preserve control, refuse dangerous actions, avoid catastrophic failures.
- **Positive alignment:** also orient models toward stable patterns that support truthfulness, autonomy, growth, context-sensitive wellbeing, and other forms of constructive human benefit.

The useful idea here is not "make the AI relentlessly positive." It is closer to:

- do not settle for "not unsafe";
- do not treat sycophancy, distraction, or shallow compliance as acceptable just because the model passed a refusal test;
- aim at behavioral attractors that are actively good for people, while preserving pluralism and agency.

That makes positive alignment relevant to several existing wiki threads:

- persona and character shaping
- truth-seeking versus sycophancy
- human agency in agent systems
- work rearchitecture and queryable organizations
- the difference between safe systems and genuinely useful systems

The hardest problem is paternalism. A system that tries to help humans flourish can easily smuggle in one institution's values, a narrow therapeutic worldview, or manipulative nudging. The strongest versions of positive alignment therefore insist on user-authored goals, polycentric governance, and context-sensitive customization.

## Why It Matters

Positive alignment gives a name to a shift already happening implicitly in frontier-lab work: models are not only being trained to avoid bad outputs, but increasingly to exhibit better epistemic habits, more constructive social behavior, stronger self-correction, and more trustworthy role behavior.
