# Quantum Interpretation Completion Schemes

Quantum interpretation completion schemes is Charli's proposed research frame:

> Quantum interpretations can be formalized as competing completion schemes over a shared empirical constraint set.

The useful move is not to say quantum interpretations are literally Cayley tables. The useful move is to borrow the discipline of completion problems: define the given entries, the missing entries, the constraints, and the criteria for a valid completion.

## Source Bridge

Dongsung Huh's 2026 arXiv open-problem paper on [Cayley-table completion](../sources/cayley-table-completion-compression.md) argues that learning systems need different inductive biases for exact discrete/algebraic rule recovery than for continuous low-rank geometric completion.

That suggests a cross-domain question:

> Can AI systems help compare how different quantum interpretations complete the same empirical pattern, and can they expose the hidden inductive biases inside those completions?

This is an analogy and a research programme, not an established result. It earns a place in the wiki because it connects the AI lane and the reality lane with a testable demand: produce formal structure, or admit it is only a metaphor.

## Completion Vocabulary

For a simplified quantum-foundations scenario:

- **Given entries:** operational predictions, observed correlations, experimental settings, outcome records, and agreed mathematical formalism.
- **Missing entries:** ontology, fact status, hidden variables, collapse dynamics, branch structure, agent-relative states, global constraints, or time-ordering story.
- **Constraints:** no-signalling, Born-rule probabilities, Bell/local-friendliness inequalities, Kochen-Specker contextuality, relativity, consistency across records, and experimental data.
- **Valid completions:** interpretations or models that preserve the agreed predictions while making explicit which assumptions they keep, reject, or modify.
- **Failure modes:** contradiction, empirical mismatch, hidden preferred structure, unfalsifiable flexibility, vague ontology, or collapse into ordinary metaphor.

## Test Cases

Good test cases are small enough to formalize and sharp enough to punish hand-waving:

- **Bell / CHSH:** given correlations and setting choices; missing entries include locality, hidden variables, measurement-setting independence, and nonlocal explanation.
- **Wigner's friend / local friendliness:** given nested-observer records and measurement choices; missing entries include observer-independent facts, universal quantum validity, and single-outcome structure.
- **Kochen-Specker contextuality:** given measurement contexts and outcome constraints; missing entries include whether values can be assigned noncontextually before measurement.
- **Delayed choice / quantum eraser:** given late measurement-context choices and interference or which-path data; missing entries include what can be said about a system's past.

## How Interpretations Look In This Frame

Different interpretations can be read as different completion strategies:

- **Everett / many-worlds:** complete by keeping universal unitary evolution and relaxing single global outcome.
- **QBism:** complete by treating quantum states as agent-relative probability assignments constrained by the Born rule.
- **Relational quantum mechanics:** complete by making states and facts relative to physical systems.
- **Objective collapse:** complete by adding real collapse dynamics.
- **Bohmian mechanics:** complete by adding definite configurations guided by a nonlocal wavefunction.
- **Global-constraint / all-at-once views:** complete by treating laws as constraints on whole histories or modal structures rather than local forward evolution.
- **Superdeterminism:** complete by relaxing measurement-setting independence, at the cost of scientific-usability worries.

This is not a ranking. It is a way to state the price each interpretation pays.

## AI Contribution

AI systems could be useful here if they are forced to operate with explicit formal criteria:

- translate a scenario into a completion problem;
- enumerate assumptions and missing entries;
- compare which assumptions each interpretation rejects;
- search for equivalent completions under different notation;
- expose hidden priors such as locality, single-outcome realism, agent-centered probability, or global law structure;
- propose new classifications of completions;
- test whether an analogy yields a constraint, theorem sketch, counterexample, or prediction.

The serious version is symbolic reasoning, theorem-proving, model search, or constrained simulation. A language model merely producing elegant comparison prose is not enough. Useful prose is a map; it is not the territory, and yes, physics will notice.

## Evaluation Standard

An AI-generated completion is useful only if it produces at least one of:

- a clearer formal constraint;
- a new classification of interpretations or no-go escape routes;
- a recoverable hidden assumption;
- a theorem sketch or counterexample worth expert checking;
- a prediction or empirical distinction;
- a proof that the analogy does not buy anything beyond existing formalism.

If it produces only vibes, it stays in the toy box.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Do not say AI can settle quantum interpretations by analogy.
- Do not say algebraic completion proves any specific interpretation.
- Do not collapse Bell, Wigner's friend, Kochen-Specker, and delayed choice into one generic "quantum weirdness" bucket.
- Do not confuse "same empirical predictions" with "all interpretations are equally disciplined."
- Do not treat a completion scheme as valid unless the constraints are explicit.
