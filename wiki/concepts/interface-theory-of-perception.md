# Interface Theory Of Perception

Interface theory of perception is Donald Hoffman's argument that perception is more like a user interface than a transparent window onto objective reality.

The desktop analogy is the useful bit. A file icon does not show voltages, filesystem structure, hardware states, or machine code. It gives the user an action-ready handle: click, drag, open, delete. Hoffman argues that perception may work similarly. The experienced world of objects, colours, distances, and surfaces may be a fitness-shaped interface rather than a literal depiction of reality as it is in itself.

That does not mean there is no world. It means the world we experience may be a compressed control surface shaped by survival, action, and species-specific capacities.

## Core Idea

- Perception is action-oriented, not truth-guaranteeing.
- Evolution selects for fitness, not necessarily for veridical representation.
- Useful perceptions may hide underlying structure in the same way desktop icons hide computer internals.
- The experienced world can be reliable enough for action without being metaphysically transparent.

For Charli's current reality lane: the kettle, table, dog, bed, and coffee can be stable interface objects without being reality's final ontology. The table still wins if you walk into it. Very democratic of it.

## Relation To Reality Threshold

[Reality Threshold](reality-threshold.md) and [Perception And Imagination Overlap](perception-and-imagination-overlap.md) ask how the brain classifies an experience as real or imagined.

Interface theory asks a different question: even when perception is stable and action-guiding, does it resemble the world as it is independently of our perceptual interface?

Together they give a useful stack:

- reality threshold: why this experience feels real rather than imagined;
- predictive/perceptual inference: how expectation and signal shape experience;
- interface theory: why the whole experienced scene may be useful without being literal;
- quantum/observer pages: why physics also resists simple view-from-nowhere fact talk.

Same broad family. Not the same theorem. Put that on a mug before the concepts start breeding in the walls.

## Relation To QBism And Physics

Hoffman is not doing QBism, relational quantum mechanics, or Adlam-style global constraints.

The connection is looser and safer:

- QBism says quantum states are agent-relative expectation structures, not God's-eye inventories.
- RQM says facts and states are relative to physical systems and interactions.
- Adlam questions whether laws must be local forward-evolution rules.
- Hoffman questions whether perception gives us a literal picture of objective reality.

They all attack naive access to finished, observer-independent reality, but from different directions.

## Conscious Realism Caveat

Hoffman also argues for conscious realism: the idea that consciousness may be fundamental and spacetime objects may be derivative.

That is much stronger than interface theory. This wiki can use Hoffman heavily for the interface/perception point without treating conscious realism as settled. The useful distinction is:

- supported here: perception is a fitness-shaped interface;
- speculative/metaphysical: reality is fundamentally a network of conscious agents.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Do not say perception is arbitrary.
- Do not say the external world does not exist.
- Do not say interface theory proves simulation theory.
- Do not say Hoffman proves consciousness is fundamental.
- Do not use "fitness beats truth" to mean "truth never matters."
- Do not confuse "not literal" with "not constrained."

The safest version is strong enough: experienced reality is a usable interface under constraint, not a raw dump of reality-in-itself.
