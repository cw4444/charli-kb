# Barenholtz - The Disappearing Ground

## Summary

Elan Barenholtz's 2026 essay, "The Disappearing Ground," argues that large language models put pressure on the old referential picture of meaning.

The essay is commentary, not a peer-reviewed paper. It was republished on Barenholtz's Substack on 2026-06-17 and notes that it first appeared in the *Institute of Art and Ideas* online magazine on 2026-05-19.

The useful claim is not that LLMs have human experience, consciousness, or biological understanding. It is sharper and less mystical: language may contain enough internal relational structure to generate useful continuations, imagery prompts, and action-guiding conditions without first mapping each word onto a fixed private essence.

## Core Argument

Barenholtz starts from a tablet analogy: if an unknown script has enough internal predictive structure that one part lets an algorithm predict another part, that would be a serious discovery even before anyone knew what the marks "meant."

He then applies the same pressure to LLMs. A language model has no direct access to redness, chairs, bodies, or rooms. It learns relations among tokens. Yet those relations are strong enough to generate fluent, context-sensitive language.

The essay calls this the **autogenerative property of language**: language has internal structure that can generate more language. LLMs did not invent that property; they exposed it.

## Meaning As Use, Not Inner Essence

The essay's strongest bridge for this wiki is its move away from meaning as a fixed hidden object and toward meaning as generative role.

"There is a chair in the living room" does not map to one private mental object, one image, or one action. In different contexts it can support different continuations:

- answering where someone can sit;
- imagining a room;
- moving furniture;
- checking whether a condition has been satisfied;
- coordinating action with another person.

The meaning is therefore not a sealed essence inside the sentence. It is the sentence's potential to generate context-appropriate language, imagery, perception checks, and behavior.

## Relation To Grounding Arguments

This is a useful counterweight to crude versions of the grounding objection.

It does not refute embodiment concerns. Human language is linked to perception, action, bodily need, social consequence, and world feedback in ways current LLMs do not duplicate.

But it does weaken the lazy claim that text-trained models are "only syntax" in a way that makes all meaning talk automatically daft. Relational structure can be usable structure. A system can preserve and exploit meaningful relations without containing a little dictionary of private essences.

## Caveats

- This is a public essay, not a technical result.
- It should not be used as proof of LLM consciousness, subjective experience, moral patienthood, or human-equivalent understanding.
- It does not erase the difference between language-only prediction and embodied living agents.
- It does not turn meaning into manifestation, private fantasy, or "whatever I feel is true."
- The useful middle is: meaning is relational and use-shaped, but use is still constrained by bodies, tools, other people, records, and the resistant world.
