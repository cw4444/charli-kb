# Libet, Schurger, Readiness Potential, And Agency

This source note tracks Charli's point about Aaron Schurger, Benjamin Libet, readiness potential, and the temptation to treat a neural precursor as proof that conscious agency is fake.

## Source Set

### Schurger, Sitt, and Dehaene 2012

Schurger, Sitt, and Dehaene's 2012 PNAS paper proposes an accumulator model for spontaneous neural activity before self-initiated movement. In Libet-style tasks, participants are told to move whenever they feel like it. Schurger's model suggests the timing of such arbitrary movements can be triggered when ongoing neural fluctuations cross a threshold.

The key move is not "free will proved." It is narrower and more useful:

- the classic readiness potential can arise from averaging neural activity before movements;
- the apparent slow buildup may reflect stochastic spontaneous fluctuations rather than a specific unconscious decision process;
- Libet-style arbitrary self-timed actions are a strange kind of action, not a full model of meaningful human agency;
- the paper explicitly says it does not prove this is the only explanation of the readiness potential.

This weakens the lazy headline "your brain decided before you did." It does not settle free will.

### Schurger et al. 2021: What is the readiness potential?

The later "What Is the Readiness Potential?" review is useful because it keeps the field honest. The readiness potential is a real EEG pattern associated with self-paced movement tasks, but its interpretation is disputed. It may reflect movement preparation, stochastic accumulation, attention, waiting, timing, or mixtures of processes depending on task design.

The review's value for this wiki is the anti-overclaim: the RP should not be treated as a clean readout of a completed unconscious decision.

### Neurofeedback and deliberate decisions

Later RP-related work also matters because readiness potentials are not equally strong across all forms of action. Some studies report smaller or absent RPs for unconscious spontaneous movements or for deliberate, value-based decisions compared with classical arbitrary Libet-style movements. That supports the cautious reading: Libet tasks are good for studying arbitrary self-paced movement, but they are a poor stand-in for real-life agency, where people deliberate, value, plan, inhibit, and revise.

## What This Adds To The Wiki

This belongs near [Practical Agency Inside Constraint](../concepts/practical-agency-inside-constraint.md), not as a triumphalist "free will is back" page.

The useful synthesis:

- agency does not require an uncaused inner ghost;
- neural precursors do not automatically erase agency;
- arbitrary button-press timing is not the same as choosing, planning, or taking responsibility;
- conscious agency may be better understood as model-guided control, inhibition, evaluation, and intervention inside constraint.

Schurger gives this wiki another anti-fatalism guardrail. Constraint is not the same as passivity. A noisy biological system can still support practical agency.

## Charli's Pessimist Gratitude Practice

Charli's bridge: Schurger accidentally creates a pessimists' gratitude practice.

Instead of forcing "positive vibes," the practice is:

> Look at all the bad things that could have happened today and did not.

This belongs with the optimism package because it is a back-door way to widen attention and register counterfactual non-events. It does not require pretending everything is good. It asks the threat-scanning brain to notice avoided harms, stable constraints, and disasters that failed to arrive.

That makes it compatible with [Optimism](../concepts/optimism.md): not denial, not manifestation, but evidence-sensitive recalibration of what the day actually contained.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Do not say Schurger disproved the readiness potential. The RP remains a real observed EEG phenomenon.
- Do not say Schurger proved free will.
- Do not say Libet was useless. His task exposed a real timing puzzle.
- Do not treat arbitrary wrist/finger movement as the model for all agency.
- Do not use neural precursors as proof that conscious life is just post-hoc theatre.
- Do not claim Schurger relied on "data Libet discarded" unless a direct source is found.
