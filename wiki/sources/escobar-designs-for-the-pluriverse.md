# Escobar - Designs For The Pluriverse

Arturo Escobar's *Designs for the Pluriverse: Radical Interdependence, Autonomy, and the Making of Worlds* is an older and more established use of "pluriverse" than the 2026 New Scientist quantum/reality framing.

The book was published by Duke University Press in 2018. Duke describes it as a work of design theory and practice that asks how design's world-making capacity could be directed toward justice, the Earth, autonomy, relationality, and more collaborative, place-based ways of living.

## Why It Matters Here

This source gives the wiki a second lineage for "pluriverse":

- the quantum/reality lineage: QBism, relational quantum mechanics, participatory realism, predictive processing, and situated perspectives;
- the decolonial/design lineage: many ways of knowing, living, making, and relating that should not be flattened into one dominant modern Western development template.

The phrase "world of many worlds" is useful because it keeps plurality concrete. It does not just mean many opinions inside one untouched background reality. It points to different practices, relationships, institutions, technologies, ecologies, and forms of life that make different worlds livable.

That makes Escobar adjacent to the wiki's reality lane, but not identical to it. His use is political, ecological, decolonial, and design-oriented. It is not a quantum interpretation, and it should not be treated as evidence for QBism, many-worlds, or predictive-processing claims.

## Useful Bridge

The safe bridge is:

Different domains are challenging one-world-from-nowhere thinking.

In quantum foundations, that pressure shows up as agent-relative states, relational facts, observer records, and limits on simple observer-independent fact talk.

In decolonial design, it shows up as resistance to universalized development, modernist design templates, and the assumption that one dominant worldview should organize every possible future.

The shared pattern is not "they are the same theory." The shared pattern is suspicion of a single detached master view that gets to define reality for everyone else.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Do not treat Escobar's pluriverse as quantum physics.
- Do not use QBism to launder decolonial design, or decolonial design to launder quantum interpretation.
- Do not turn "world of many worlds" into private fantasy or manifestation.
- Do not flatten the political/ecological content into a decorative metaphor.
- Do not treat "many worlds" here as Everettian branching.

Same phrase, different job. Useful precisely because the difference matters.
