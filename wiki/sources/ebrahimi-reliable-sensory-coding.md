# Ebrahimi et al. - Reliable Sensory Coding Despite Cellular Variability

Sadegh Ebrahimi and colleagues published *Emergent reliability in sensory cortical coding and inter-area communication* in *Nature* in 2022.

## Access Note

Nature marks the paper closed/hybrid access, not open access. The public page exposes the complete abstract, metadata, references, and figure previews. This is therefore an abstract/metadata synthesis; any subscription PDF remains private.

## Sidebar: Volatile Cells, Reliable Population Code

The problem is beautifully rude: individual sensory neurons can vary substantially from day to day, yet visual discrimination remains reliable.

The team imaged more than 21,000 neurons across eight mouse neocortical areas, repeatedly over five days, while mice performed a visual-discrimination task. The public abstract describes a time-resolved sequence:

- at rest, the neocortex showed one functional-connectivity pattern;
- within about 200 ms of stimulus onset, inter-area connections reorganized around shared task-related activity;
- during a short roughly 300 ms state, inter-area sensory transmission and redundancy of sensory coding both peaked;
- by around 0.5 s, the visual population representation had become stable despite large day-to-day changes in individual-cell responses;
- around 1 s, a global fluctuation mode carrying the mouse's upcoming response appeared across all measured areas and remained orthogonal to modes carrying sensory information.

The useful lesson is not that individual neurons do not matter. It is that reliable representation can be a property of redundancy, population geometry, and coordinated inter-area communication rather than a fixed cast of individually reliable cells.

## Why It Matters Here

This is a compact companion to [Representational Geometry In Brains And LLMs](representational-geometry-brains-and-llms.md). Both it and the BLA paper weaken the naive hunt for one neuron with one stable label. In Ebrahimi et al., the same perceptual information can remain usable when individual cellular responses drift because the relevant structure is preserved at a population/network level.

It also adds a useful boundary for active-perception work: state-sensitive and context-sensitive sensory cortex is not necessarily unreliable sensory cortex. The system can reorganize rapidly and still preserve a stable task-relevant representation.

## Do Not Overclaim

- This is a mouse visual-discrimination experiment, not direct evidence about human visual experience, memory, consciousness, or personal identity.
- The paper does not show that perception is perfectly stable; it identifies conditions under which task-relevant population coding was robust to cellular variability.
- Orthogonal modes in this analysis do not prove that sensory information and planned action are universally separate in all brain systems.
- Population robustness is not a license to call every unstable individual measurement irrelevant.
