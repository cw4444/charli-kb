# White House Advanced AI Innovation And Security Order

On 2026-06-02, the White House published the Executive Order [Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/promoting-advanced-artificial-intelligence-innovation-and-security/).

This is a durable AI-governance and security source. It creates a federal posture around "advanced AI models" and "covered AI companies" while explicitly trying to avoid mandatory licensing or government preclearance of frontier-model releases.

## What The Order Does

The order frames advanced AI as both a strategic opportunity and a security risk. It says the United States should promote innovation, voluntary collaboration, and security testing while protecting against cyber, chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear, and other high-consequence risks.

The operational pieces matter more than the rhetoric:

- defines **covered AI companies** using compute thresholds for training advanced models;
- directs NIST to develop a voluntary capability-maturity framework for covered companies;
- directs NIST to create a process for classified cyber benchmark testing of advanced AI models;
- directs federal agencies to offer voluntary pre-deployment evaluations for covered companies;
- creates a program of trusted early-access partners who can receive access to unreleased or not-yet-public advanced models to find vulnerabilities;
- directs creation of an AI Cybersecurity Information Clearinghouse;
- directs federal work on using advanced AI for vulnerability scanning, patch development, and software supply-chain security;
- directs cyber testing and vulnerability scanning of Department of War systems and federal civilian systems using advanced AI;
- requires reports on chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear, and cyber capabilities of advanced models.

In plain English: the order tries to make frontier-model testing and AI-assisted cyber defense into federal operating machinery.

## The Innovation Boundary

The order repeatedly says it does not create mandatory licensing, premarket approval, or required pre-deployment testing for AI model release. It positions the federal role as voluntary evaluation, classified benchmarking, trusted-access programs, standards, clearinghouses, and procurement or coordination support.

That boundary is important. This is not the EU AI Act model. It is a U.S. executive-branch approach that tries to combine frontier-model security testing with a pro-innovation posture.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Do not treat an Executive Order as durable legislation. It is executive-branch policy and can be changed, challenged, limited, or superseded.
- Do not say this creates mandatory AI licensing. The order explicitly says it does not.
- Do not treat voluntary pre-deployment evaluations as proof that all frontier releases will be safe.
- Do not assume classified benchmarking will be transparent to the public.
- Do not collapse U.S., EU, G7, and foundation approaches into one governance model. They overlap, but they are not the same thing.
- Do not treat "AI-assisted cyber defense" as automatically safe. The same capability pressure can matter offensively.
