# Le Cunff et al. - ADHD Traits And Curiosity

Anne-Laure Le Cunff, Charlotte Russell and Eleanor J. Dommett published "Hyperactive-impulsive ADHD traits predict higher curiosity in adults: evidence from a cross-sectional study" in *BMC Psychology* on 2026-04-10. The version of record is listed as 2026-05-18. The article is open access under CC BY 4.0, and the dataset is available through King's Open Research Data System.

## What They Did

The study used a cross-sectional online survey of 521 adults living in the United Kingdom. Just over half of the sample reported a formal ADHD diagnosis, with additional participants reporting self-identified ADHD or no ADHD diagnosis.

The authors measured ADHD traits with the Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale v1.1 (ASRS-v1.1), separating inattention and hyperactivity-impulsivity. They measured trait curiosity with the Curiosity and Exploration Inventory-II (CEI-II), including both stretching (seeking knowledge and new experiences) and embracing (willingness to engage with novelty and uncertainty).

## What They Found

ADHD trait scores showed small-to-moderate positive correlations with overall curiosity and its subdimensions. Participants with ADHD reported moderately higher curiosity than participants without an ADHD diagnosis.

The key result is the split between ADHD trait dimensions. In regression models controlling for age, gender and education, hyperactivity-impulsivity uniquely predicted curiosity, while inattention did not. The authors report this as consistent across diagnostic groups.

The authors interpret the result as initial evidence that hyperactive-impulsive ADHD traits may be associated with higher self-reported curiosity in adults. They connect this to accounts of hypercuriosity, novelty seeking, exploration, reward sensitivity and engagement with uncertainty.

## Why It Matters

This source is useful because it refuses the purely deficit-shaped version of ADHD without pretending the difficulties vanish. The point is not "ADHD is secretly a superpower." The point is that some traits commonly framed as dysregulation may also relate to exploratory drive.

For the wiki, the clean bridge is curiosity as a cognitive engine. A mind that is pulled toward novelty, uncertainty and unresolved questions may build unusually wide knowledge trails. That can produce real synthesis when paired with structure, source checks and finishing habits. Without those brakes, the same curiosity can become tab-sprawl, priority collapse and novelty-chasing in a hat.

## Caveats

- Cross-sectional design: no causal direction can be inferred.
- All ADHD and curiosity measures were self-reported.
- Recruitment was online and explicitly described as ADHD-and-curiosity research, so self-selection is likely.
- The sample was predominantly women, White and highly educated.
- The study does not show that curiosity produces functional advantages in learning, work or wellbeing.
- Hyperactivity-impulsivity predicted self-reported curiosity; it did not prove "ADHD curiosity" as a single mechanism.
- Curiosity can be a strength and a management problem at the same time. Both facts may rudely coexist.
