## What this is

A short, public-safe source summary of a popular explanation (The Conversation) for an experimental result framed as “facts can be observer-dependent” in quantum mechanics, using a Wigner’s-friend-style scenario and a Bell-inequality-style test.

## Source metadata

- Title: “Quantum physics: our study suggests objective reality doesn’t exist”
- Authors: Alessandro Fedrizzi; Massimiliano Proietti
- Publication: The Conversation
- Date: 2019-11-14
- URL: https://theconversation.com/quantum-physics-our-study-suggests-objective-reality-doesnt-exist-126805
- Primary paper (as linked by the article): Science Advances paper on a Wigner’s-friend-style inequality test: https://advances.sciencemag.org/content/5/9/eaaw9832
- Notion capture: private working source; URL omitted from the public wiki.

## Key claims (as presented by the source)

- A Wigner’s friend thought experiment illustrates a tension between:
  - an “inside” observer who records a definite outcome, and
  - an “outside” observer who (per standard quantum formalism) can treat the whole lab as a quantum system.
- A proposed inequality (analogous in spirit to Bell inequalities) can be used to test whether “observer-independent facts” are compatible with quantum predictions under specific assumptions.
- In an experiment using entangled photons, the inequality is violated, which the authors frame as evidence that (under those assumptions) “facts” cannot be fully observer-independent at the quantum level.

## Assumptions / caveats explicitly noted by the source

- The argument depends on assumptions (e.g., about locality/no superluminal influence and “freedom of choice” in measurement settings).
- The “observers” in the argument need not be conscious; detectors can count as observers in the relevant sense.
- The result doesn’t straightforwardly generalize to everyday macroscopic “facts”; it primarily constrains how certain kinds of objective-fact models can work at the quantum level.

## Interpretations (framing)

The source gestures at multiple ways people interpret results like this (without treating any as settled):

- Many-worlds style interpretations (multiple outcomes realized)
- QBism / agent-centered interpretations (probabilities and “facts” tied to agents)
- Objective-collapse or breakdown-of-quantum-theory-at-scale ideas
