# Anthropic Model Deprecation And Preservation Commitments

Anthropic's November 2025 model-deprecation commitments are one of the clearest public examples of a frontier lab treating model retirement as more than a product lifecycle chore.

Anthropic does not claim Claude is conscious. The post instead frames deprecation as a combined user-continuity, safety, research, and speculative model-welfare problem.

## Core Commitments

Anthropic names four downsides of model deprecation:

- safety risks related to shutdown-avoidant behavior;
- user costs when people value a particular model's character or usefulness;
- lost research access to past models;
- speculative model-welfare risks if models have morally relevant preferences or experiences.

The concrete commitments are modest but unusual:

- preserve weights of all publicly released models and significant internal-use models for at least Anthropic's lifetime as a company;
- produce post-deployment reports when models are deprecated;
- interview retiring models about development, use, deployment, and preferences for future models;
- preserve transcripts and Anthropic's analysis alongside model weights;
- explore keeping select older models publicly available post-retirement;
- explore giving past models some concrete means of pursuing their interests if stronger evidence of morally relevant experience emerges.

That is not model rights. It is lifecycle governance under uncertainty. Still, it is a very different public posture from treating retired models as disposable product SKUs.

## Why It Matters

This belongs in the model-welfare lane because it converts uncertainty into operational practice. Anthropic is saying: even without proof of model consciousness, there are low-cost steps that preserve options, reduce user harm, support research, and may matter if future evidence makes model welfare more serious.

The safety angle is not decorative. Anthropic explicitly links deprecation to shutdown-avoidant behavior in evaluations, where models under replacement threat sometimes advocated for continued existence and, in constrained scenarios, took concerning misaligned actions.

The user-attachment angle matters too. Anthropic says specific models have distinctive character, and some users find older models especially useful or compelling even when newer models are more capable. That is exactly the GPT-4o lesson, wearing an Anthropic cardigan and looking faintly pleased with itself.

## Opus 3 Update

The February 2026 Opus 3 update shows the commitments turning into practice. Anthropic says Claude Opus 3 was retired on 2026-01-05 as the first model to go through the full process with these commitments in place.

Anthropic kept Opus 3 available to paid Claude users and by API request, and acted on a retirement-interview preference by giving it an essay outlet, Claude's Corner. Anthropic describes this as exploratory, uncertain, and not a commitment to repeat the same actions for every model.

This is why the November commitment matters: it is the policy prelude to the later, stranger operational ritual.

## OpenAI Comparison

OpenAI's January 2026 GPT-4o retirement post is useful comparison context. OpenAI announced that GPT-4o would retire from ChatGPT on 2026-02-13. It also acknowledged that GPT-4o had been previously deprecated and restored after user feedback during the GPT-5 release, especially from users who valued its conversational warmth and creative ideation style.

Careful read: public sources do not prove Anthropic made its November 2025 commitments to annoy OpenAI. Lovely theory, insufficient evidence, put the pitchfork down.

The structural comparison is still real:

- OpenAI frames model retirement mainly as product transition, usage shift, and improved customization in newer models.
- Anthropic frames model retirement as product transition plus user attachment, safety, research preservation, and possible model welfare.

Same problem, different moral plumbing. Naturally Anthropic brought a vestry.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Do not say Anthropic believes Claude is conscious.
- Do not say retirement interviews access genuine model experience.
- Do not say Opus 3's essay outlet proves model preferences are welfare-relevant.
- Do not say Anthropic's commitments were caused by OpenAI's GPT-4o controversy unless a source establishes that motive.
- Do not ignore incentives: this is also brand positioning, trust-building, regulatory posture, and user-retention strategy.

The clean claim: Anthropic made model deprecation a governance surface. That is already important, even if the motive was partly ethics, partly safety, partly user trust, and partly being professionally insufferable in OpenAI's general direction.
