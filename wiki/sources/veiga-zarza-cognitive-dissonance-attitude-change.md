# Veiga-Zarza et al. - Cognitive Dissonance And Attitude Change

Estrella Veiga-Zarza, Fátima Álvarez, Eddie Harmon-Jones, and Luis Carretié published *Neural basis of attitude change motivated by cognitive dissonance: a scoping review* online on 2026-02-10, later appearing in the June 2026 issue of *Cognitive, Affective, & Behavioral Neuroscience*.

## Access Note

The Springer page labels the journal hybrid and this article as no-access / pay-per-view. It exposes the abstract, metadata, references, figure previews, and article bibliography, but not the full HTML or PDF to an unauthenticated reader. This note is therefore limited to public abstract/metadata synthesis. A subscription PDF may be read privately, but is not copied into the public wiki.

## Core Claim

Cognitive dissonance theory proposes that meaningful inconsistency between cognitions can generate negative affect and motivate changes in belief, attitude, or behaviour that reduce the experienced incoherence.

The authors conducted a PRISMA-ScR scoping review of studies that met three criteria:

- compared high- and low-dissonance conditions;
- quantitatively measured attitude change;
- used brain recording or stimulation methods.

Their synthesis proposes a rough, sequentially useful model:

1. **Conflict/dissonance detection:** the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) is especially implicated in detecting the relevant inconsistency.
2. **Attitude/value updating:** dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) activity appears linked to updating the value representation of the stimulus.
3. **Preference representation:** the update may affect regions encoding stimulus preference, including ventral striatum.

The review flags the anterior insula and precuneus as unresolved rather than settled circuit nodes. Good. The brain remains inconveniently complicated.

## Why It Matters Here

This gives the wiki a mechanism-shaped account of a familiar human move: an action, commitment, effort, social role, or self-description can make an old attitude expensive to maintain. Rather than simply confessing "I was wrong," a person may revise which option looks valuable, reasonable, or self-consistent.

That is not proof that people consciously lie to themselves. It is a reminder that valuation and self-model maintenance can be active processes. The important question is whether updating restores contact with evidence or merely makes the internal story more comfortable.

It connects to:

- [Practical Agency Inside Constraint](../concepts/practical-agency-inside-constraint.md): agency includes noticing when a goal, action, or identity is distorting the evidence used to justify it.
- [Salience Weighted Judgment](../concepts/salience-weighted-judgment.md): conflict can make some information emotionally and cognitively urgent, then bias what gets reweighted.
- [Pessoa - The Entangled Brain](pessoa-entangled-brain.md): attitude revision is not cleanly separable into a rational-cognition stage followed by an emotional aftertaste; conflict, value, body state, motivation, and control can interact.

## Do Not Overclaim

- This is a scoping review, not a meta-analysis or a single causal experiment.
- The public note rests on abstract/metadata, not the subscription full text.
- ACC, DLPFC, and ventral striatum are not single-function "dissonance," "rationality," and "preference" buttons.
- Cognitive dissonance is not a universal explanation for all belief change, hypocrisy, political disagreement, or awkwardness.
- Attitude change is not automatically epistemic improvement. Sometimes updating corrects an error; sometimes it rationalizes a commitment.
