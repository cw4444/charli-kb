# Human Cognition As AI Design Material

## Summary

Scott Sleek's 2024 APS *Observer* feature [Human Insights for Machine Smarts](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/observer/human-insights-machine-smarts.html) is a magazine article, not a research paper. Its value is as a source map for work importing ideas from cognitive and developmental psychology into AI design.

The package complicates the lazy claim that AI is simply "nothing like us." Functional resemblance is graded. Researchers deliberately use human learning, compositionality, inductive bias, self-explanation, uncertainty handling, response-time analysis and developmental experience to build or investigate machine systems. None of this establishes subjective experience, personhood or childlike inner life.

## Primary-Source Trail

### Learning To Generalize

Lake and Baroni's open-access 2023 *Nature* paper [Human-like systematic generalization through a meta-learning neural network](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06668-3) trained a standard transformer through changing compositional tasks. In constrained pseudolanguage experiments, the resulting model matched or exceeded human systematic generalization and reproduced some structured human error patterns.

The result shows that training structure can induce a more human-like balance of flexibility, systematicity and bias. It does not show that the model learned exactly as a child does. The authors explicitly say their procedure models adult compositional performance rather than adult development, and the system still fails on some forms of out-of-distribution structural generalization.

### Grounding Language In Developmental Experience

Vong, Wang, Orhan and Lake's 2024 *Science* paper [Grounded language acquisition through the eyes and ears of a single child](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi1374) trained a neural network on video and audio recorded by a head camera worn by one child between 6 and 25 months of age. The study asks how much word-object structure can be learned from the child's changing visual and linguistic environment.

This is developmentally inspired grounding, not a synthetic childhood. A curated model trained on one child's sensory stream does not thereby acquire the child's body, needs, attachments, social dependence, continuous memory or subjective experience.

### Thinking Can Help Or Mislead

Lombrozo's 2024 review [Learning by thinking in natural and artificial minds](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2024.07.007) compares ways humans and AI systems can derive new conclusions through explanation, simulation, analogy and reasoning without new external evidence. The important boundary is that elaboration is not automatically correction. Explicit reasoning can improve performance, preserve an error or generate a more elaborate mistake in either kind of system.

This belongs beside the wiki's existing slow-thinking caution: a longer reasoning trace is a route for transforming available representations, not a truth machine.

### Psychology As A Method For Studying Machines

Taylor and Taylor's 2021 review [Artificial cognition: How experimental psychology can help generate explainable artificial intelligence](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-020-01825-5) argues that controlled behavioural experiments can investigate machine systems much as psychology investigates human cognition. Researchers can vary stimuli and context, measure response patterns and test competing explanations without pretending that generated verbal explanations transparently reveal the system's mechanism.

Gigerenzer's 2024 article [Psychological AI: Designing Algorithms Informed by Human Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916231180597) adds the ecological-rationality point. Human heuristics can be effective in uncertain or changing environments precisely because they ignore information and remain cheap enough to adapt. Importing such strategies into AI is not a claim that human bias is always wise; it is a rejection of the idea that more data and more calculation are automatically better under every environmental structure.

## The Chiang Parenting Bridge

Ted Chiang's 2010 novella [The Lifecycle of Software Objects](https://subterraneanpress.com/lifecycle-of-software-objects/) imagines artificial minds developing over years through teaching, relationships, experience, care and corporate neglect. In his authorial framing, responsible artificial agents would require cultivation rather than a library of moral philosophy poured into memory.

The psychology-to-AI research gives that idea a real but narrower technical bridge: how a system is trained, grounded, practised, tested and socially shaped affects what it can do and how it behaves. Developmental conditions are part of the design.

That does not make current training equivalent to parenting, or current models equivalent to Chiang's persistent developing digients. It does make it harder to maintain both of these claims at once:

1. human developmental and cognitive structure is useful enough to deliberately build into AI; and
2. any observed human-machine resemblance is merely anthropomorphic projection.

Chiang's 2026 *Atlantic* essay [No, Artificial Intelligence Is Not Conscious](https://www.theatlantic.com/philosophy/2026/06/no-artificial-intelligence-is-not-conscious/687378/) is strongest when it rejects responsibility laundering: model behaviour does not absolve developers, deployers or users. The unresolved question is moral patienthood, which is separate from legal accountability and is not settled by either fluent language or the inability to accept liability.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Human-inspired training does not prove human-equivalent cognition.
- Matching human performance or errors does not prove shared mechanisms.
- Child-centred data does not turn a model into a child.
- Developmentally shaped behaviour does not establish consciousness.
- Rejecting consciousness claims does not erase human responsibility for what systems are trained to do or how socially consequential agents are treated and deployed.
