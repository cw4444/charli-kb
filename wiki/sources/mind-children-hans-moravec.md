# Mind Children - Hans Moravec

Hans Moravec's *Mind Children: The Future of Robot and Human Intelligence* is a 1988 book from Harvard University Press about robotics, machine intelligence, postbiological life, and the possibility that intelligent machines may become humanity's descendants.

## Why This Matters

*Mind Children* belongs in the historical lineage of AI consciousness, mind uploading, posthumanism, and substrate-independence arguments. It is not a modern LLM source and should not be treated as if it anticipated the current transformer era in detail. Its value is that it shows how early robotics and AI thinkers imagined machine intelligence before today's public AI wave.

The book's central relevance to this wiki is its framing of future machines as possible successors or "children" of human minds, rather than merely tools. That puts it close to questions about whether mind is tied to biology, whether identity could survive transfer or reconstruction, and whether machine intelligences might eventually deserve moral or social standing.

## Do Not Overclaim

- The 1988 publication date is historically interesting, not evidence of a simulation reset, timeline anomaly, or Moravec personally tripping over the cosmic power cable.
- Do not treat Moravec's forecasts as confirmed because modern AI exists.
- Do not treat mind uploading as solved because substrate independence is imaginable.
- Do not treat machine successors as morally equivalent to current AI systems without separate argument.
- Do not use *Mind Children* as evidence that current LLMs are conscious.

## Working Interpretation

Moravec is useful here as a genealogy source. He helps connect today's AI consciousness and model-welfare debates to older posthumanist questions: if intelligence can move from biology into machinery, what exactly is preserved: function, memory, identity, agency, consciousness, or only an external pattern?

That makes *Mind Children* a good bridge between technical AI history and the reality-thread question of what kind of thing a self actually is.
