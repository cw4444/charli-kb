# Kahneman And Tversky - Judgment Under Uncertainty

## Why This Matters

Daniel Kahneman's most useful role in this wiki is not "famous economist-psychologist man." It is the recurring warning that felt importance, felt probability, and felt confidence are not the same thing as evidence.

The Nobel summary is the broad public anchor: Kahneman received the 2002 economics prize for integrating psychological research into economic science, especially human judgment and decision-making under uncertainty. His work with Amos Tversky is the spine behind much of modern heuristics-and-biases research and behavioral economics.

## Core Ideas

### Availability

Tversky and Kahneman's 1973 paper defines availability as judging frequency or probability by the ease with which examples come to mind. Availability is often useful because memorable examples can track real frequency, but it is also affected by vividness, recency, emotional charge, repetition, and how easy an example is to retrieve.

That is the serious research cousin of the everyday "frequency illusion" rabbit hole. If something has just become salient, it can start appearing everywhere. The world may not have changed much. The retrieval and attention system has.

Useful wiki translation: do not trust "I keep seeing this" until you have checked whether the base rate changed, your attention changed, the feed changed, or the search terms changed. Google is an availability machine with a search bar on it. Lovely. Dangerous.

### Heuristics And Biases

The 1974 Science paper gathered the broader claim: under uncertainty, people often use simplifying heuristics such as representativeness, availability, and anchoring. These shortcuts are not random stupidity. They are efficient rules that often work well enough, but they generate systematic errors in predictable conditions.

This belongs beside the wiki's agency and reality lanes because it gives a non-mystical account of why a smart person can be confidently wrong: the mind substitutes a tractable question for a harder one. "How likely is this?" becomes "how easily can I picture or recall this?" "How true is this?" becomes "how coherent does this story feel?"

### Prospect Theory

Kahneman and Tversky's 1979 prospect theory paper is the decision-under-risk anchor. It describes choices around gains and losses relative to a reference point, rather than only final wealth or objective expected utility.

The important wiki version:

- losses often loom larger than equivalent gains;
- people can be risk-averse in gains and risk-seeking in losses;
- framing a situation as a loss or a gain can change decisions;
- the same objective outcome can feel different depending on the reference point.

This is relevant to optimism because risk response is not just "positive" or "negative." A person trying to avoid a felt loss may take larger risks than they would take to secure an equivalent gain. Human motivation, once again, refuses to behave like a clean spreadsheet. Very inconsiderate.

### Focusing Illusion

Schkade and Kahneman's 1998 California paper and Kahneman et al.'s 2006 Science paper are the happiness-and-attention anchors. The focusing illusion is the tendency to overestimate the effect of one currently salient factor on overall life satisfaction or experienced well-being.

The durable line for this wiki is: what you are thinking about gets overweighted. Income, location, status, health, relationships, model access, a frightening AI conversation, one paper, one diagnosis, one humiliation, one opportunity - when it is in focus, it can feel like the master variable.

That does not mean the factor is irrelevant. It means attention inflates it. This is the practical boundary Charli was circling with "nothing is as important as you think it is while you are thinking about it." The useful response is not to dismiss the concern. It is to widen the frame before making decisions.

## Why It Connects To This Wiki

Kahneman's work belongs here because it is a source-backed bridge between perception, belief, agency, optimism, and AI use:

- [Optimism](../concepts/optimism.md): optimistic or pessimistic futures can be made vivid and overweighted; calibration matters.
- [Reality Threshold](../concepts/reality-threshold.md): felt realness and felt importance are constructed signals, not direct access to the world.
- [Practical Agency Inside Constraint](../concepts/practical-agency-inside-constraint.md): agency improves when people know which parts of judgment are biased by salience, framing, and loss response.
- [Cognitive Latency Shock](../concepts/cognitive-latency-shock.md): fast AI search and synthesis can intensify availability by making one thread instantly expandable.

For AI work, this is a useful steering rule: a fluent model can make a topic feel complete, urgent, or newly everywhere. That may be insight. It may also be availability plus velocity. Slow down, ask what evidence changed, and keep the receipts.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Heuristics are not defects in stupid people. They are ordinary shortcuts that can be adaptive.
- Availability is not the same thing as the Baader-Meinhof frequency illusion, though they are practically adjacent.
- The focusing illusion does not mean money, location, illness, model access, or life events do not matter. It means single factors get overweighted when attention is narrowed onto them.
- Prospect theory is descriptive, not a moral theory of how people should decide.
- Kahneman's work does not say human judgment is hopeless. It says unaided confidence is a poor quality-control system.
