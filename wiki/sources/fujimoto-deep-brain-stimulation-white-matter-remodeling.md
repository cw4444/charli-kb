# Fujimoto et al. - Deep Brain Stimulation And White Matter Remodeling

Satoka H. Fujimoto, Atsushi Fujimoto, Catherine Elorette, Adela Seltzer, Emma Andraka, Keondre Herbert, Gaurav Verma, William G. M. Janssen, Lazar Fleysher, Davide Folloni, Ki Sueng Choi, Brian E. Russ, Helen S. Mayberg, and Peter H. Rudebeck published [Deep brain stimulation induces white matter remodeling and functional changes to brain-wide networks](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02301-4) in *Nature Neuroscience* on 2026-06-01.

The paper matters here because it makes deep brain stimulation look less like a simple on/off electrical override and more like an intervention that can alter white matter microstructure and brain-wide network communication.

## Core Claim

The authors targeted deep brain stimulation to white matter near the subcallosal anterior cingulate cortex in macaques. This subcallosal cingulate DBS target models a treatment approach used for some people with treatment-resistant depression.

After six weeks of chronic stimulation, the authors report:

- selective increased fractional anisotropy in the cingulum bundle;
- increased myelinating oligodendrocytes in the mid-cingulum bundle;
- increased myelination measured with electron microscopy;
- altered functional connectivity between the subcallosal cingulate region and broader brain networks, especially default-mode-network-related communication.

The durable point: DBS may act through both rapid functional circuit effects and slower white matter remodeling. For mood disorders, that matters because the treatment target is not only local gray matter activity; it is also the white matter wiring through which large-scale mood-related networks communicate.

## Why It Matters

This source belongs in the neuroscience lane as a neuromodulation and plasticity anchor.

It is adjacent to the wiki's optimism, sleep, and mood threads, but it should not be collapsed into them. Sleep, mood, future expectation, and optimism probably interact through broad affect-regulation and brain-state loops. This paper adds a harder biological reminder: some mood-relevant interventions may work by changing network dynamics and myelinated white matter structure, not only by changing thoughts, expectations, or surface-level affect.

In plain English: sometimes the system is not merely thinking differently. Sometimes the cable routing and insulation are part of the problem. Helpful. Horrifying. Very brain.

## Do Not Overclaim

- This is a macaque study with very small animal numbers.
- The macaques were healthy, not depressed humans.
- This does not prove that DBS works for every person with treatment-resistant depression.
- This does not imply that ordinary mood, optimism, or sleep can be reduced to myelin changes.
- This is not a home neuromodulation protocol. Do not turn "brain stimulation exists" into gadget optimism with wires.
- DBS is an invasive clinical intervention, not a lifestyle tool.
