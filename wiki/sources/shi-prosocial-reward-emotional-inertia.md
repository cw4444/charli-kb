# Shi et al. - Prosocial Reward And Negative Emotional Inertia

Hongqiao Shi and colleagues published *Sustained dorsolateral prefrontal cortex response to prosocial reward predicts lower real-world negative emotional inertia* online in 2025, later appearing in the June 2026 issue of *Cognitive, Affective, & Behavioral Neuroscience*.

## Access Note

Springer marks the article hybrid/no-access. This note uses the public abstract and metadata only; Charli's subscription PDF remains private and is not reproduced here.

## Core Finding

In 61 young adults (46 women; ages 18–25), the researchers combined fMRI during a monetary gambling task with two weeks of experience sampling of everyday emotional states.

They compared repeated pro-self reward with prosocial reward (money for charity). The public abstract reports:

- prosocial and self-reward showed different neural-habituation patterns in left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) and right inferior temporal gyrus;
- participants with **weaker habituation** — a more sustained left-DLPFC response — to prosocial reward tended to show **lower negative emotional inertia** in daily life, meaning negative states were less sticky across successive measurements;
- the authors explicitly warn that the prosocial response was relatively weak, so the difference should be interpreted cautiously.

## Why It Is Interesting, And Why It Is Not A Sermon

This is a narrow reward-dynamics association, not evidence that charity, kindness, spiritual practice, or being a very good person cures low mood.

The durable question is more interesting: when a type of reward remains neurally engaging rather than quickly habituating, does that predict more flexible daily emotional dynamics? This paper offers one small, correlational, young-adult answer for prosocial reward and negative affect.

It belongs near [Optimism](../concepts/optimism.md) as a reward/emotion-dynamics neighbour, and near [Pessoa - The Entangled Brain](pessoa-entangled-brain.md) as a reminder that valuation, social meaning, control, and affective dynamics are entangled. It is not direct optimism evidence and does not justify any intervention recommendation.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Small sample; restricted age range and gender balance; correlational association.
- Two weeks of experience sampling is more ecologically interesting than a single lab rating, but remains self-report and does not measure every aspect of emotion.
- Weak prosocial responses limit confidence in the group contrast.
- Sustained DLPFC response is not a moral virtue marker, a measure of altruism, or a treatment target.
- Lower negative emotional inertia is not proof of better mental health in every context; some persistence can reflect a stable response to a stable problem.
