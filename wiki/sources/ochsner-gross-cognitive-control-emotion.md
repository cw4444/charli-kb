# Ochsner And Gross - Cognitive Control Of Emotion

Ochsner and Gross's 2005 review is the heavyweight foundation for the respectable version of "change the interpretation, change the feeling." It belongs in this wiki because it gives the emotion-regulation lane a proper cognitive-neuroscience anchor: attention, appraisal, reappraisal, distancing, and response modulation can shape emotional experience and expression.

This is not the head key from *Locke & Key*. You are not unlocking your skull and lifting out the parts you dislike. The useful claim is smaller and better: the meaning assigned to a situation, the attention given to it, and the regulatory strategy applied to it can change how the emotional response unfolds.

## Why It Matters

This source is the older scaffold under several later wiki threads:

- [Van Dijk et al.](van-dijk-emotional-time-travel.md) show an affective-forecasting version: people can overestimate future anger and sadness partly because they under-model later regulation, reappraisal, acceptance, or rumination.
- [Optimism](../concepts/optimism.md) uses the same bridge when positive future simulation or positive interpretation works as regulation rather than denial.
- [Manifestation As Behavior Modification](../concepts/manifestation-as-behavior-modification.md) needs this boundary: "feel it real" is useful only when it changes attention, appraisal, action, and persistence while staying in contact with evidence.
- [Salience Weighted Judgment](../concepts/salience-weighted-judgment.md) needs the control-side complement: salience is not only what lands loudly; regulation can change which interpretation gets weight next.
- [Pessoa's network framing](pessoa-entangled-brain.md) is the brake on cartoon neuroscience. Do not reduce this to a tiny executive manager in prefrontal cortex yanking an amygdala lever.

## Useful Translation

The plain-English version:

- attention can change which parts of a situation become emotionally load-bearing;
- appraisal can change what the situation means;
- reappraisal can reinterpret the same facts in a way that changes emotional force;
- distancing can reduce immediate self-involvement;
- suppression or response modulation can change expression, though that is not the same as cleanly changing the underlying feeling;
- emotion regulation is work done by a whole system, not a magic switch.

For Charli's practical-agency lane, this matters because it makes emotional prediction and emotional identity less fixed than they feel. A forecast can be wrong not because the event is harmless, but because future-you is not just present-you trapped at the moment of impact. Future-you may reinterpret, contextualize, accept, problem-solve, distract, recover, or, yes, ruminate like an absolute professional.

## Boundaries

- This was checked through public metadata and indexing records; a reliable public full-text PDF was not found in this pass.
- Treat it as a foundational 2005 review, not the final state of the field.
- Cognitive control is not total control. Regulation can fail, cost effort, depend on context, or become avoidance and denial when misused.
- Reappraisal is not "everything is fine." It is a change in interpretation, and bad interpretations can be more comfortable without being more accurate.
- This is not a clinical protocol, medical advice, or evidence that affirmations alter external reality.
