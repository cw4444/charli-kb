# Pluriverse, QBism, And Predictive Reality

This source note follows Charli's pointer to New Scientist's paywalled March 2026 article, "Forget the multiverse. In the pluriverse, we create reality together."

The article itself should not be copied into the wiki. Public metadata and newsletter text are enough to identify the idea: New Scientist framed the pluriverse as a move away from one external, observer-independent reality toward reality "continually created and woven out of innumerable perspectives." Treat that as a discovery trail, not as a source to quote or reproduce.

### Cavalcanti X screenshot: experience and memory

- Type: local screenshot of public X posts

- Access note: X search did not reliably surface the original post during research; Charli later supplied the direct URL. The screenshot is preserved locally under `raw/` and should not itself be published.

The screenshot captures Eric Cavalcanti writing: "Some experiences happen that are not remembered. Some experiences are remembered that never happened."

This is not presented as a technical claim from a paper. Its value here is as a public-commentary bridge between first-person experience, memory, and the repo's existing observer/reality lane. Cavalcanti is already relevant to this wiki through Wigner's-friend and local-friendliness work, where the question is whether observer records can be treated as one absolute event ledger.

The memory line belongs near [Reality Threshold](../concepts/reality-threshold.md), but it is not the same claim. Dijkstra's reality-threshold work asks how an experience is judged real or imagined. Cavalcanti's line points to a neighboring problem: whether an experience is preserved as memory, lost to memory, or apparently remembered despite not having occurred.

Together they give a useful ladder:

- perception: what signal is experienced now;
- reality monitoring: whether the signal is judged real or imagined;
- memory: whether the experience is later preserved, distorted, or confabulated;
- observer records: whether different agents' records can be fused into one public ledger.

Do not use this as Mandela-effect bait. The sober point is already enough: first-person experience, memory, and public record are not the same thing.

In *Black Mirror* season 7 episode 2, "Bete Noire," Maria is the only person who remembers a prior version of events after Verity uses a pendant-linked system to move through realities where Verity's claims have always been true. Netflix's official explainer frames the episode as a workplace dark comedy about gaslighting, bullying, revenge, and different memories or truths. The episode's fictional "quantum compiler" is useful here because it dramatizes the nightmare version of memory/public-record mismatch: one person retains continuity while the social world and records disagree.

This belongs in the wiki only as a cultural reference. It is not evidence for many-worlds, QBism, reality shifting, or personal manifestation. It is a neat horror-story version of the same practical stack: experience, memory, public record, and world-model can come apart.

### Fuchs: participatory realism and QBism

Christopher Fuchs's "On Participatory Realism" is the cleanest primary anchor. Fuchs groups QBism with other first-person or observer-participatory quantum views and argues that these views are not simple antirealism. The core claim is that reality is richer than any complete third-person description can capture.

Fuchs and Schack's "QBism and the Greeks" gives the short technical line: a quantum state is not an element of physical reality; it is an agent's personal probability assignment about future experience. Fuchs and Stacey's "QBism: Quantum Theory as a Hero's Handbook" expands the same direction: subjective probability, quantum measurement as action, Bell violations as a lesson about nature's creativity, and SIC measurements as the technical side of QBism.

Fuchs and Stacey's 2025 "QBism, Polishing Some Points" is especially useful for the current wiki because it states the live QBist position in a more recent form:

- the Born rule is normative, not a descriptive law of nature;
- probabilities do not tell nature what to do;
- quantum states have no ontic hold on the world;
- measurement outcomes are personal experiences for the agent enacting them;
- QBism should be distinguished sharply from Everett / many-worlds.

### Glick: QBism and scientific realism

David Glick's "QBism and the limits of scientific realism" is a useful secondary check because it asks whether QBism can still count as realism. The paper frames QBism as agent-centered and first-personal: quantum theory constrains agents' subjective degrees of belief rather than giving a God's-eye description of reality.

For this wiki, Glick helps keep the pluriverse idea from sliding into "nothing is real." A QBist or participatory-realist view can reject a single view-from-nowhere without denying resistance, surprise, shared practice, or intersubjective science.

### Predictive processing / predictive coding

The predictive-processing connection is not quantum physics. It is a brain-level neighbor.

Friston and Kiebel's predictive-coding paper treats perception as inference over internal models that explain sensory data. Andy Clark's *Surfing Uncertainty* frames the brain as an engine of probabilistic prediction, with hierarchical predictive processing as the mechanism. Nearby predictive-processing reviews describe perception as top-down prediction plus bottom-up prediction error.

The useful bridge is this: perception is not passive reception of a finished world-picture. It is model-guided contact with a world that pushes back. That rhymes with the pluriverse frame, but it does not prove QBism and it does not make neuroscience into quantum foundations.

## What The Pluriverse Adds

"Pluriverse" is useful in this wiki as a bridge term:

- not many separate Everett branches;
- not a deterministic block universe viewed from nowhere;
- not private fantasy or manifestation;
- a shared reality built from interacting, situated perspectives under constraint.

This fits the existing reality lane because the wiki already has pages on [QBism and global constraints](../../themes/qbism-adlam-observer-dependent-reality.md), [many-worlds](../../themes/many-worlds-and-observer-dependent-reality.md), [relational quantum mechanics](../../themes/rovelli-relational-quantum-mechanics-and-reality.md), [interface theory of perception](../concepts/interface-theory-of-perception.md), and [reality threshold](../concepts/reality-threshold.md).

The Cavalcanti screenshot adds a memory-level caution to the same stack: even after experience happens, the later remembered record can fail in both directions. Experiences can go unremembered, and remembered experiences can be false. This does not make reality optional. It means "what happened," "what was experienced," "what was remembered," and "what can be publicly checked" are different layers.

## Bounded Synthesis

The safe synthesis is:

Reality may not be best understood as a complete, observer-independent inventory. Quantum foundations, relational interpretations, QBism, and predictive neuroscience all pressure that picture from different directions.

But the pressure is not uniform:

- QBism makes quantum states and probabilities agent-relative.
- Many-worlds keeps a universal wavefunction and pays for it with branch-relative outcomes.
- Relational quantum mechanics makes facts system-relative.
- Adlam-style global constraints question local forward-evolution as the only form of law.
- Predictive processing says perception is model-mediated and continually updated.

The pluriverse is the umbrella mood, not the theorem.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Do not copy or paraphrase the paywalled New Scientist article closely.
- Do not say Fuchs has abandoned QBism unless there is a direct primary source showing that.
- Do not say the pluriverse proves consciousness creates physical reality.
- Do not collapse pluriverse into many-worlds. They point in nearly opposite directions: many-worlds multiplies branch structure from one universal wavefunction; pluriverse emphasizes interlocking first-person perspectives.
- Do not use predictive processing as evidence for quantum interpretation. It is an analogy and a cognitive-neuroscience neighbor.
- Do not use "personal reality" to mean arbitrary reality. The world resists bad models. Tables continue their important public service of hurting shins.
- Do not use memory fallibility as an excuse to dissolve public evidence. Memory is corrigible partly because records, other agents, and the world push back.
