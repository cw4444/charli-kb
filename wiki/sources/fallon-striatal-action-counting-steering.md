## Core Claim

Fallon et al. ask how the basal ganglia organize complex voluntary behavior when an animal must both move through space and perform a required number of actions.

The team trained mice on an operant counting task: the animal had to make a specific number of lever presses to obtain a reward. That allowed the authors to measure two things at once:

- continuous kinematics, meaning how the mouse moved and steered
- discrete action count, meaning progress through the required press sequence

The public abstract reports a dissociation between direct-pathway and indirect-pathway striatal projection neurons:

- activating direct-pathway neurons steered mice contraversively and extended press sequences
- activating indirect-pathway neurons steered mice ipsiversively and prematurely terminated press sequences

Calcium imaging found direct- and indirect-pathway neurons whose activity tracked either physical approach or count progress. Some activity patterns ramped in ways consistent with accumulation and discharge dynamics.

The authors' useful synthesis is that the difference between direct- and indirect-pathway population activity scaled with proximity to both spatial and numerical goals. In plain English: the basal ganglia can act like a push-pull controller that integrates where the animal is going with how far through the action sequence it is.

## Why It Matters Here

This is a strong addition to the wiki's agency lane because it makes goal-directed behavior less mystical and less slogan-shaped.

Agency is not just "deciding." It can involve:

- counting progress through an action sequence
- steering the body through space
- comparing current state with a goal state
- extending or terminating an action run
- integrating continuous movement with discrete action units

That fits [Practical Agency Inside Constraint](../concepts/practical-agency-inside-constraint.md): useful action emerges from constrained machinery that tracks progress, applies corrections, and changes what happens next.

It also fits the [Neuroscience](../../themes/neuroscience/overview.md) lane: action is implemented by circuits, bodies, timing, sequence structure, and feedback, not by a free-floating intention blob wearing a philosophical hat.

## Bridge Terms

- `Action counting`: tracking progress through a required number of discrete actions.
- `Goal-directed steering`: continuous movement adjustment toward a spatial or task goal.
- `Direct pathway / indirect pathway`: striatal output pathways often framed as opponent control systems, though the adult version is richer than "go" and "stop."
- `Push-pull controller`: a control-system interpretation where opposing pathway activity can bias movement direction, action continuation, and sequence termination.
- `Accumulation and discharge`: ramping activity that may track progress toward a threshold or goal, then reset or drop after completion.

## Do Not Overclaim

- This is a mouse study, not direct evidence about human willpower, habits, ADHD, Parkinson's disease, or everyday agency.
- The public wiki note is based on abstract/metadata, not full-text extraction.
- Direct and indirect pathways should not be flattened into cartoon "go" and "stop" buttons.
- Action counting in a lever-press task is not the same as human planning, language, moral choice, or complex deliberation.
- Goal-directed steering is embodied motor control, not proof that agency is only movement.
- The paper supports a mechanistic bridge for action control; it does not settle free will, consciousness, or responsibility.
