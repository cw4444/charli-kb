# Hershfield - Future Self-Continuity

## Source

Hal E. Hershfield, [Future self-continuity: how conceptions of the future self transform intertemporal choice](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1749-6632.2011.06201.x), *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences*, 1235, 30-43, 2011. PMID: [22023566](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22023566/); PMCID: [PMC3764505](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3764505/).

Access note: public NIH author manuscript in PMC; publisher version via Wiley. This page summarizes the review in original words.

## Why It Earns The Shelf Space

This is the missing empirical and theoretical foundation beneath several existing wiki claims about future identity. Adlerian [Fictional Finalism](../concepts/fictional-finalism.md) says imagined future goals can organize present behaviour. Hershfield supplies a narrower decision-science mechanism: a delayed consequence matters less when the person who will receive it feels psychologically remote, indistinct, or unlike the present self.

The paper is a review, not one decisive experiment. Its durable contribution is to organize early work around three aspects of the represented future self:

- **similarity or connectedness:** how much future-you feels like a continuation of present-you;
- **vividness:** how concretely and realistically that person can be imagined;
- **positivity:** how positively the future self is regarded.

The proposal is not that vivid thoughts summon outcomes. It is that the represented recipient of a delayed benefit can change how much weight that benefit receives now.

## Evidence Reviewed

### Similarity And Connectedness

Hershfield and colleagues adapted an overlapping-circles measure of self-other closeness so participants could indicate how similar they felt to themselves ten years later. Greater reported overlap correlated with choosing more larger-later rewards in temporal-discounting tasks. In a working-adult sample, future-self similarity also correlated with accumulated assets after controls including age and education.

Other studies reviewed in the paper manipulated perceived identity stability. Participants told that core identity tends to remain stable reported more overlap with their future selves and made more patient choices than participants told that identity changes substantially.

These results mix correlational and experimental evidence. They support future-self connectedness as one contributor to intertemporal choice; they do not make it the sole cause of saving, health, or self-control.

### Neural Evidence

In an fMRI study reviewed by Hershfield, current-self judgments produced stronger activity than future-self judgments in cortical midline regions associated in that literature with self-referential processing. Larger current-versus-future differences were associated with steeper discounting of future rewards.

This is often flattened into "the brain treats your future self as a stranger." The careful version is weaker: in that task and sample, future-self judgments partly resembled other-person judgments, and individual differences in the neural contrast correlated with choice. A regional activation contrast is not proof that the brain literally classifies future-you as a stranger, nor does it establish one neural cause of shortsighted behaviour.

### Vividness And Age-Progressed Selves

The best-known experiments used age-progressed images or avatars to make an older self more concrete. Participants exposed to their aged selves allocated more hypothetical money to retirement and showed more patience than controls who saw current selves or another person's aged avatar. Follow-up web studies also found higher hypothetical retirement allocations after exposure to neutral age-progressed images, reducing the likelihood that a smiling face alone drove the result.

These studies show that representation can alter choices under experimental conditions. Most outcomes were hypothetical allocations or laboratory intertemporal choices, not verified decades-long saving behaviour. Age progression may also change salience, aging attitudes, demand expectations, or emotion alongside future-self vividness.

### Positivity

The positivity branch is less secure than the paper's tidy three-part summary can make it look. Direct ratings of liking and caring for the future self did not robustly predict intertemporal choice, partly because responses showed little variation.

The review therefore drew on more indirect evidence: country-level associations between attitudes toward older people and national saving, perspective-taking exercises involving an older person, respect-for-the-elderly writing prompts, and semantic priming. Some of these manipulations increased larger-later choices, but respect for older people is not identical to affection for one's own future self. The national comparison is also ecological and cannot establish individual causation.

## Later Clarification

Bixter and colleagues' 2020 open-access study argued that **future self-identification** is a clearer umbrella term than future self-continuity. Their point is exact: continuity properly concerns relatedness across temporal selves, whereas vividness and positivity can vary independently of that continuity.

Across three samples of first-year undergraduates, they found relatedness, vividness, and positivity were correlated but distinguishable factors, with different relationships to wellbeing, imagery, self-control, and academic outcomes. That supports keeping Hershfield's triad as a useful map while refusing to treat it as one settled mechanism or one interchangeable score.

## Limits

- This is a 2011 narrative review of an early research programme, not a preregistered meta-analysis.
- Several prominent studies came from Hershfield and close collaborators; the review is an important primary synthesis, not an independent systematic assessment of the programme.
- Much of the evidence used student samples, self-report measures, hypothetical monetary choices, brief primes, or laboratory tasks.
- Correlations with assets or neural activation do not establish causation.
- Similarity, vividness, and positivity were not jointly and cleanly manipulated in the reviewed work; Hershfield explicitly described their relationships as largely unknown.
- Feeling unlike a future self is not automatically irrational. Expected identity change, uncertainty, mortality, material scarcity, and present need can all alter the value of waiting.
- Future-oriented choice is not always the morally or practically superior choice. Present-you is also a person, irritatingly enough.
