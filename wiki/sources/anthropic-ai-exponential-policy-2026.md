# Anthropic AI Exponential Policy 2026

## Source Metadata

- Source: Dario Amodei, [Policy on the AI Exponential](https://darioamodei.com/post/policy-on-the-ai-exponential)

## Summary

Dario Amodei's essay and Anthropic's paired policy frameworks mark a shift from "transparency and optionality" toward proposed binding regulation and economic transition planning.

The core argument is that AI capability is moving on an exponential timescale while legislation moves slowly. Anthropic says transparency laws were useful when the shape of risk was still unclear, but that recent frontier-model evidence, especially cyber capability and AI-assisted AI development, now justifies a stronger government role.

The package has two halves:

- an Advanced AI Framework for catastrophic-risk governance of frontier models;
- an Economic Policy Framework for labor-market disruption and benefit-sharing.

## Advanced AI Framework

Anthropic proposes obligations for large frontier developers whose models exceed a training-compute threshold and whose companies cross major AI revenue or R&D spending thresholds. The public summary says the framework would apply to models trained using more than `10^25` FLOPs, developed by companies earning more than `$500 million` in AI-related revenue or spending more than `$1 billion` on AI R&D.

The covered risk areas are:

- biological weapons risk;
- cyber risk;
- loss-of-control risk;
- automated R&D that could amplify those risks.

The framework recommends mandatory testing, public summaries, safety frameworks, system cards, regular risk reports, critical-incident reporting, independent evaluation, security programs for weights and training infrastructure, and government authority to block or deter deployments that pose unacceptable catastrophic risk.

The important policy shift is the enforcement claim. Anthropic is no longer only asking developers to disclose safety practices. It is asking for a legal regime with teeth, while trying to limit the scope to specific catastrophic-risk categories and preserve state authority outside those functions unless a strong federal law exists.

## Economic Policy Framework

Anthropic's economic framework treats AI-driven labor disruption as uncertain but serious enough to plan around now.

It lays out responses for three rough unemployment scenarios:

- around `5%` unemployment: expanded capital accounts, training grants, licensing reform, wage insurance, and incentives for firms to retain and redeploy workers;
- around `10%` unemployment: expanded unemployment insurance, sector-specific transition support, basic-needs relief, and possible incentives for gradual rollout;
- unprecedented unemployment: broader income replacement and new revenue or benefit-sharing mechanisms such as basic income, sovereign-wealth models, or equity-sharing structures.

Anthropic says it is committing `$350 million` alongside the framework:

- `$200 million` for an Economic Futures Research Fund;
- `$150 million` for a national fellowship program aimed at helping early-career people extend AI benefits to communities across the United States.

The framework is careful to say financial support is necessary but not sufficient because work also carries dignity, meaning, and social structure. That belongs beside this wiki's [Agentic Work Rearchitecture](../concepts/agentic-work-rearchitecture.md) and [Knowledge Collapse](../concepts/knowledge-collapse.md) lanes: the problem is not only GDP. It is whether people retain agency, learning, security, and a place in the social machinery.

## Why It Matters

This is timeline-worthy because it ties together several Anthropic threads already tracked here:

- Anthropic's compute, capital, and talent scale-up;
- model-character and safety framing;
- public concern as democratic accountability rather than a marketing failure.

The durable signal is not that Anthropic has solved AI governance. It is that a frontier lab is putting its own money and political capital behind a policy package that says transparency alone is no longer enough.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Do not treat Anthropic's policy package as neutral public-interest analysis. It is a company proposal from a frontier lab with its own incentives.
- Do not say the frameworks are law. They are proposals.
- Do not say the economic-disruption scenarios are forecasts. Anthropic frames them as planning cases.
- Do not treat `$350 million` as solving labor displacement. It is a funding commitment attached to research and fellowships, not a safety net.
- Do not flatten the policy into "stop AI." Anthropic argues for continued innovation under testing, evaluation, security, and deployment authority.
- Do not ignore the self-serving edge: binding rules can protect the public, but they can also shape competition, compliance burdens, and which labs can afford to operate.
