# OpenAI Industrial Policy For The Intelligence Age

This page records OpenAI's 2026 policy paper, *Industrial Policy for the Intelligence Age: Ideas to Keep People First*, and the related "fund" language Charli remembered.

The short version: OpenAI did publish a sweeping industrial-policy paper on 2026-04-06. It proposes a **Public Wealth Fund**, but that is a policy proposal, not an already-created OpenAI fund. Separately, OpenAI says it is establishing a pilot program of fellowships and focused research grants of up to `$100,000` and up to `$1 million` in API credits for work building on the policy ideas.

## What OpenAI Published

OpenAI's announcement frames the document as an early, exploratory set of people-first policy ideas for the transition toward superintelligence. The company says incremental policy updates will not be enough and invites feedback through `newindustrialpolicy@openai.com`.

The PDF is not a technical paper or product launch. It is a public-policy agenda. The main argument is that AI capability may move from minute- and hour-scale tasks toward month-scale projects, reshaping work, institutions, power, and wealth distribution.

Source:

- [OpenAI: Industrial policy for the Intelligence Age](https://openai.com/index/industrial-policy-for-the-intelligence-age/)
- [OpenAI PDF: Industrial Policy for the Intelligence Age](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/561e7512-253e-424b-9734-ef4098440601/Industrial%20Policy%20for%20the%20Intelligence%20Age.pdf)

## The Fund Question

There are three nearby ideas that are easy to muddle:

### 1. Proposed Public Wealth Fund

The April 2026 paper proposes creating a Public Wealth Fund so citizens, including people not invested in financial markets, can share in AI-driven economic growth. OpenAI says policymakers and AI companies should work together on how to seed such a fund, which could invest in long-term assets tied to AI companies and broader AI adoption.

This is the thing Charli half-remembered as "money into a fund." It matters, but keep the wording exact: it is a proposed public wealth fund, not proof that OpenAI has already put money into a citizen-dividend fund.

### 2. OpenAI Fellowships And Research Grants

OpenAI's announcement and the end of the PDF say OpenAI is establishing a pilot program of fellowships and focused research grants of up to `$100,000`, plus up to `$1 million` in API credits, for work that builds on these and related policy ideas.

That is the concrete OpenAI-backed funding program attached to the industrial-policy paper.

### 3. OpenAI For Countries National Startup Funds

OpenAI's earlier 2025 OpenAI for Countries initiative separately says partner countries and OpenAI could raise and deploy national startup funds using local and OpenAI capital to seed national AI ecosystems.

That is related to OpenAI's larger "Intelligence Age" public-private infrastructure posture, but it is not the same as the April 2026 Public Wealth Fund proposal.

Source:

- [OpenAI: Introducing OpenAI for Countries](https://openai.com/global-affairs/openai-for-countries/)

## Main Policy Ideas

The paper's useful signal is not one magic proposal. It is the whole cluster:

- broad access to useful AI and baseline capability;
- worker voice in AI deployment;
- startup and small-business support;
- tax-base modernization as AI shifts income from labor toward capital and corporate returns;
- a Public Wealth Fund;
- faster grid and energy infrastructure;
- efficiency dividends, including 32-hour or four-day workweek pilots without loss of pay;
- adaptive safety nets and portable benefits;
- pathways into care, education, health, and community work;
- AI-enabled scientific discovery infrastructure;
- safeguards for high-consequence domains;
- provenance, verification, auditing, incident reporting, containment, and public input mechanisms.

## Compute Follow-Through

OpenAI's 2026-04-29 "Building the compute infrastructure for the Intelligence Age" post belongs next to this policy paper. It says Stargate is OpenAI's effort to build the compute foundation for AGI, that OpenAI had already surpassed its January 2025 goal of securing 10GW of US AI infrastructure by 2029, and that more than 3GW had been added in the previous 90 days.

That post makes the policy paper feel less abstract: OpenAI is arguing for industrial policy while also building large-scale compute infrastructure, local community programs, energy and construction partnerships, and a broader infrastructure ecosystem.

Source:

- [OpenAI: Building the compute infrastructure for the Intelligence Age](https://openai.com/index/building-the-compute-infrastructure-for-the-intelligence-age/)

## Why It Matters

This is timeline-worthy because OpenAI is not only shipping models and agents. It is publicly arguing that superintelligence requires a new economic and institutional settlement: wealth-sharing mechanisms, tax changes, public-private infrastructure, workforce transition supports, AI access, and safety institutions.

That belongs in this wiki's AI/agent lane because it connects model capability, work rearchitecture, compute infrastructure, and political economy. It also belongs next to Anthropic's capital/compute signal: frontier labs are now talking like infrastructure companies, industrial-policy actors, and public-institution designers, not just model vendors.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Do not say OpenAI created a Public Wealth Fund. It proposed one.
- Do not say the fellowship/grant pilot is the same as the Public Wealth Fund.
- Do not treat OpenAI's policy paper as neutral analysis. It is a company proposal from a frontier lab with obvious interests.
- Do not treat the paper as democratic legitimacy. OpenAI says the ideas should be refined, challenged, or chosen through democratic processes.
- Do not treat the compute-infrastructure post as proof that all local energy, water, labor, and community concerns are solved.
- Do not collapse "AI will reshape work" into a single deterministic labor-market forecast.
