# QBism, Global Constraints, and Observer-Dependent Reality

This note compares two adjacent but different attacks on simple classical realism:

- **QBism:** quantum states are agent-relative probability assignments about an agent's possible future experiences.
- **Emily Adlam's global-constraint / all-at-once direction:** some laws may be better understood as constraints on whole histories or modal structure, not as step-by-step evolution from earlier to later states.

Working hypothesis: both views resist a naive external "view from nowhere," but they do so differently. QBism makes the quantum-state description agent-relative. Adlam's constraint view pushes against the idea that physical explanation must always be local, forward-evolving, and time-directed.

## Short Plain-English Summary Of QBism

QBism says the quantum state is not a little physical object, a hidden script, or God's-eye description of the system. It is an agent's disciplined betting manual: a compact way for that agent to assign probabilities to possible experiences they may have after acting on the world.

The Born rule still matters, but not as a mechanism that takes an objective wavefunction and spits out objective chances. In QBism, the Born rule is a normative constraint on an agent's expectations. It tells an agent how their probability assignments should hang together if they are using quantum theory coherently.

Key source already in the wiki: Fuchs and Schack's "Quantum-Bayesian Coherence" in the [Source Index](../sources/source-index.md#fuchs-and-schack-2013---quantum-bayesian-coherence).

For the newer bridge language around "pluriverse," see [Pluriverse](../wiki/concepts/pluriverse.md) and [Pluriverse, QBism, And Predictive Reality](../wiki/sources/pluriverse-qbism-predictive-reality.md). Use that term as connective tissue for interlocking situated perspectives, not as a claim that Fuchs has abandoned QBism or that predictive neuroscience proves quantum interpretation.

## Short Plain-English Summary Of Adlam's All-At-Once / Global-Constraint View

Adlam's relevant work asks whether modern physics gives us reason to loosen the standard picture where laws always push a present state forward into the future. Quantum mechanics, relativity, retrocausal models, and temporal nonlocality all create pressure on that picture.

Her "laws as constraints" proposal treats laws as modal constraints: rules about what whole patterns are physically possible, including cases where the rule is nonlocal, atemporal, all-at-once, retrocausal, or otherwise not well described as ordinary time evolution. In nearby global-determinism work, some quantum features may be perspectival effects: local observers access only part of a larger global pattern of correlations.

This is block-universe-adjacent because it takes whole-history structure seriously. But it should not be flattened into a slogan like "the future causes the past" or "observers create reality." The safer reading is: some explanations may be global constraints on the entire spacetime pattern rather than local pushes from past to future.

Key sources added to the [Source Index](../sources/source-index.md#adlam-2022---laws-of-nature-as-constraints): Adlam 2018 on temporal nonlocality, Adlam 2018 on global determinism, Adlam 2021 on quantum foundations, and Adlam 2022 on laws as constraints.

## How QBism Treats The Observer / Agent

QBism puts the agent in the formal story, but not as a magic consciousness field.

- An agent takes an action on the external world.
- A measurement outcome is personal to that agent: it is part of that agent's experience.
- A quantum state represents that agent's expectations, not an observer-independent physical state.
- Different agents may assign different quantum states because they have different information, plans, and experiences.
- Interpersonal agreement is not automatic in the formalism; it has to be explained through communication, shared records, and ordinary scientific practice.

This makes QBism a natural neighbor of [Wigner's Friend](wigners-friend.md). The friend and Wigner need not assign the same quantum state, because a quantum state is not a shared object in the world. It is the state of an agent's expectations.

## How Adlam Treats Time, Laws, And Global Structure

Adlam's relevant move is about lawhood and explanation, not primarily about subjective experience.

- Laws need not always be forward-evolution rules.
- Some laws may constrain an entire history "all at once."
- Temporal locality should not be assumed just because it is familiar.
- Retrocausal and atemporal models can be treated as serious law structures rather than as violations of scientific order.
- A global pattern may explain local correlations without a local step-by-step mechanism available inside time.

This sits near [Bell Inequalities](bell-inequalities.md), [Local Friendliness](local-friendliness.md), and superdeterminism-adjacent debates because many no-go theorems depend on assumptions about locality, free choice, and the independence of measurement settings. A global-constraint picture may reject the idea that correlations must be explained only by past common causes or local causal propagation.

## Where They Touch Observer-Dependent Reality

Both views disturb the simple idea that reality is one public database viewed from nowhere.

- QBism: the quantum state is agent-relative, and outcomes are personal experiences for the agent.
- Adlam: the local time-evolution picture may be perspectival or incomplete; local agents may see only a slice of a larger globally constrained structure.
- Both are compatible with the idea that physical description depends on standpoint, role, access, or context.
- Both push against naive classical realism, where facts are simply there with all properties fixed independently of measurement context and temporal structure.

But the contact is limited. QBism is directly about agent-relative quantum-state assignment and measurement experience. Adlam's global-constraint work is about the metaphysics and structure of laws, time, and possible histories.

## Where They Clearly Differ

| Question | QBism | Adlam / global constraints |
|---|---|---|
| Main target | Objective quantum states and impersonal quantum probabilities | The assumption that laws must be local, temporal evolution rules |
| Role of observer | Central: agents act, assign probabilities, and have experiences | Not central in the same way: observers are local systems inside a globally constrained structure |
| What becomes relative | Quantum states, probabilities, and measurement outcomes-for-an-agent | Local explanatory perspective on a whole-history pattern |
| Relation to time | Not primarily a theory of time | Directly concerned with temporal locality, retrocausality, and atemporal/all-at-once lawhood |
| Metaphysical flavor | Personalist, pragmatist, agent-centered realism | Structural, modal, global, block-universe-adjacent realism |
| Measurement problem strategy | Collapse is an agent's belief update after experience | Measurement may need explanation inside a larger nonstandard law structure, but this is not the same move as QBist updating |

## Does Either View Support "Reality Depends On Observers"?

**QBism:** yes, but only in a precise and limited sense. The quantum state and the outcome-as-experienced are agent-relative. QBism does not say the external world is invented by the observer, nor that consciousness psychically manufactures particles. It says quantum theory is a tool used by agents to manage expectations about their interactions with the world.

**Adlam:** not in the same sense. Her global-constraint work can support the idea that a local observer's account is perspectival and incomplete. It can also support the idea that local events should be understood within a whole-history structure. But it is not an observer-dependence view in the QBist sense. The global structure is not created by observers.

Careful claim: both views resist an absolute, classical, external "view from nowhere." Only QBism makes the agent's experience foundational to the interpretation of quantum states and outcomes.

## Wigner's Friend And Local Observer Independence

QBism connects directly to [Wigner's Friend](wigners-friend.md), [Observer-Independent Facts](observer-independent-facts.md), and [Local Friendliness](local-friendliness.md):

- The friend can have an outcome as their personal experience.
- Wigner can assign a quantum state to the lab from outside.
- There is no requirement that the friend's experienced outcome and Wigner's quantum-state assignment be fused into one observer-independent ledger before communication.
- This offers a clean way to reject "absoluteness of observed events" without treating the friend as unreal.

Adlam's global-constraint view connects more indirectly:

- Local-friendliness and Bell-style arguments depend on locality, local agency, and independence-style assumptions.
- Global constraints may challenge the assumption that the relevant correlations must be explained by locally evolving causes.
- A block-universe-adjacent view can make "the whole experimental arrangement" explanatorily prior to local event-by-event storytelling.
- This does not automatically solve Wigner's friend; it changes the background picture of what counts as a satisfying physical explanation.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Do not say QBism proves consciousness causes collapse.
- Do not say QBism is idealism. It is agent-centered and experience-centered, but it still assumes an external world that resists and surprises agents.
- Do not say Adlam proves the universe is a simulation, a dream, or mind-dependent.
- Do not say global constraints are simply "future causes past." Retrocausal language may be useful in some models, but Adlam's broader point is about law structures that are not reducible to ordinary forward time evolution.
- Do not collapse QBism and Adlam into the same view. Their overlap is anti-naive-realism, not identical metaphysics.
- Do not treat Wigner's-friend experiments as proof that everyday reality is subjective. The existing wiki caveat still stands: these results constrain bundles of assumptions under idealized quantum-foundations scenarios.

## Relation To Idealism, Simulation Theory, And Consciousness-Causes-Collapse

Neither view directly supports simulation theory. Neither view requires Berkeleyan idealism. Neither view, as summarized here, establishes that consciousness physically collapses the wavefunction.

QBism is easiest to misread because it uses "agent" and "experience" as core terms. The important distinction is:

- Supported: quantum states encode an agent's expectations about possible experiences.
- Not supported: the agent's mind creates all of physical reality.

Adlam is easiest to misread because "all-at-once" and "block universe" language sounds mystical. The important distinction is:

- Supported: physical law may constrain whole histories rather than evolve local states step by step.
- Not supported: the world is a scripted illusion for observers.

## Charli's Working Interpretation

This is interpretation/speculation, not an established result:

QBism and Adlam's global-constraint direction look like two different ways of breaking the same classical habit.

- QBism breaks the habit that a quantum state must be a God's-eye inventory of the world.
- Adlam breaks the habit that explanation must be a little causal machine pushing the present into the future.
- Together, they suggest that "reality" may be less like a single public spreadsheet and more like a stable interface between agents, records, constraints, and perspectives.

But this is a bridge for thinking, not a physics conclusion. The safe version is: quantum foundations gives several independent reasons to be suspicious of simple observer-independent fact talk, and different interpretations locate the problem in different places.

For the agency question, see [Practical Agency Inside Constraint](../wiki/concepts/practical-agency-inside-constraint.md). The useful bridge is that constraint does not automatically mean passivity. Even inside a globally constrained or relation-dependent picture, local agents can still improve their models, identify live interventions, and test which changes propagate.

## Open Questions

- Can QBism recover enough intersubjective stability for ordinary public science without reintroducing observer-independent facts through the back door?
- In global-constraint pictures, how should local agency/free choice be understood without collapsing into untestable superdeterminism?
- Are QBist agent-relative outcomes and Brukner-style relational objectivity compatible, or only superficially similar?
- Does Adlam's structural/modal realism give a better home for local-friendliness violations than standard causal pictures?
- What exactly counts as an "agent" in QBism: a human, an organism, a detector, an AI system, or any system with action/experience-like structure?
- What exactly counts as a "local perspective" in a global-constraint theory?
