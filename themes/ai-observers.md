# AI Observers

AI-as-observer is not a standard result of Bell or Wigner's-friend physics. It is a speculative extension. The safe way to ask the question is operational:

> What physical and informational role must a system play to count as an observer in a quantum-foundations argument?

## Observer Roles In The Formal Arguments

In modern Wigner's-friend and local-friendliness work, an observer usually needs to:

- interact with a system,
- produce a durable record,
- condition later actions or inferences on that record,
- be treatable, at least in principle, as part of a larger quantum system.

This does not require consciousness in the formalism.

## Where AI Might Fit

An AI system could count as an observer in a weak operational sense if it:

- receives measurement data,
- stores a record,
- updates a model or policy,
- communicates or acts based on the record.

But that does not mean current AI systems create quantum facts, collapse wavefunctions, or have special metaphysical status. They are physical information-processing systems embedded in ordinary decohering environments.

## The Useful Question For Charli

The interesting bridge is not "is ChatGPT conscious enough to collapse reality?" The interesting bridge is:

> If facts in quantum theory can be agent-relative, what kind of agent is required for a fact to be relative to it?

Possible dimensions:

- memory,
- causal coupling,
- model update,
- agency,
- communication,
- self-reference,
- embodiment,
- participation in a shared verification practice.

## Speculative Boundary

AI observers are philosophically relevant but not experimentally established in this literature. Keep this page tagged as speculative until tied to specific papers on quantum observers, artificial agents, or quantum information protocols.
