# Local Friendliness

Local friendliness is a no-go framework developed by Bong, Cavalcanti, Wiseman, Pryde, and collaborators. It strengthens the Wigner's-friend/Bell connection by using assumptions that are intended to be weaker and more operational than traditional Bell local realism.

## Core Assumptions

The key assumptions are usually framed as:

- Absoluteness of observed events: an observed event is not merely relative; if it happens for one observer, it happens for all.
- Local agency: agents can freely choose interventions that are not correlated with relevant events outside their future light cone.

From these assumptions one derives local-friendliness inequalities. Quantum theory can violate them.

## Difference From Bell

Bell's theorem is often summarized as targeting "local realism," though that phrase can hide details. Local friendliness shifts the focus:

- Bell asks whether outcomes can be locally explained by hidden variables.
- Local friendliness asks whether observed events themselves are absolute while local agency remains intact.

This makes the theorem especially relevant to observer-dependent reality. It is about facts and observed events, not merely hidden microscopic properties.

## Experimental And Practical Limits

The strongest local-friendliness arguments often assume superobservers can perform coherent measurements on whole laboratories, including records made by friends. This is straightforward for small quantum systems but not for ordinary human observers.

So the practical reading should be:

- Strong formal result under idealized assumptions.
- Experimentally suggestive in small systems.
- Not a direct demonstration that human memories can be put into coherent superposition and reversed.

## Recent Extensions

Recent work studies local-friendliness polytopes, multipartite scenarios, and links with Kochen-Specker noncontextuality. This matters because it tests whether local friendliness is genuinely new or another face of contextuality.
