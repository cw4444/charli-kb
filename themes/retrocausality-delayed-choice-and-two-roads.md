# Retrocausality, Delayed Choice, and Two Roads

This page compares Wheeler's delayed-choice experiment with Emily Adlam's "Two Roads to Retrocausality" and nearby retrocausal work.

Short version: Wheeler makes the past look strangely dependent on the measurement context we choose later. Adlam asks a cleaner foundations question: if quantum theory and relativity already strain ordinary forward-only causal explanation, which route to retrocausality is actually worth taking?

The useful distinction is this:

- Wheeler's delayed choice is a measurement-context argument about what we are licensed to say about a quantum system's past.
- Adlam's two roads paper is a locality/time/lawhood argument about whether retrocausal or temporally nonlocal explanations are motivated by Bell, relativity, and nonlocality.

Do not flatten either into "the future literally edits the past." That is the breakfast-table version. It is also how people end up in nonsense before the kettle has boiled.

## Wheeler's Delayed Choice

Wheeler's delayed-choice setup asks whether a photon "came by one route" or "both routes" when the experimental arrangement that distinguishes particle-like from wave-like behavior is chosen only after the photon has already entered the apparatus.

In the interferometer version:

- with the final beam splitter absent, the setup can reveal which route the photon took;
- with the final beam splitter present, interference behavior is recovered;
- the choice can be delayed until after the photon has already travelled through the earlier part of the apparatus.

Wheeler's strongest rhetorical move is not that the future sends a signal backward. It is that quantum theory denies us a context-free story of what the photon "really did" before the whole experimental phenomenon is registered.

In "Law Without Law," Wheeler pushes this into a broader participatory-universe picture: observation and registration help define what can be said about past events. That makes Wheeler important for this wiki's observer-dependent-reality lane. But he is more philosophical and programmatic than theorem-level.

## What Delayed Choice Does And Does Not Show

Delayed-choice experiments show that a late measurement context can determine whether the experiment supports a which-path description or an interference description.

They do not, by themselves, prove:

- backward-in-time signalling;
- that consciousness collapses the wavefunction;
- that the past can be freely rewritten;
- that retrocausality is the only interpretation.

Standard quantum mechanics predicts the outcomes without requiring usable messages to the past. Everett, QBism, relational quantum mechanics, Bohmian mechanics, consistent histories, and operational Copenhagen-ish views can all tell different stories about the same experimental pattern.

The safe Wheeler claim is: the past of a quantum event is not always a detachable classical itinerary that exists independently of the measurement arrangement used to close the phenomenon.

## Adlam's Two Roads To Retrocausality

Adlam's paper separates two motivations for retrocausality.

Road 1: use retrocausality to rescue locality.

This route says Bell correlations look nonlocal only because we assumed hidden variables or ontic states are independent of future measurement settings. If future settings can help constrain earlier hidden variables, then a model may preserve local mediation in spacetime while relaxing the usual arrow-of-time assumption.

Adlam is skeptical that this is the best motivation. Her argument is that the common reasons for preferring local retrocausal models over nonlocal ones are not strong enough. If the price of locality is a global or all-at-once structure, locality may no longer be doing the explanatory work people wanted from it.

Road 2: accept nonlocality, then avoid a preferred reference frame.

This is Adlam's preferred route. Bell-style nonlocality plus relativity creates a problem: if there is no preferred foliation or frame, different observers can disagree about which spacelike-separated measurement happened first. If we insist on treating the nonlocal relation as real without sneaking in a hidden preferred time-ordering, the explanation starts to look temporally nonlocal or retrocausal in a broader sense.

On this road, retrocausality is not a little particle carrying news from tomorrow. It is a sign that the whole pattern of spacetime events may be constrained in a way that is not well described by step-by-step forward evolution.

## Adlam's All-At-Once Preference

Adlam argues that "all-at-once" retrocausality is more coherent than a dynamical picture where influences physically travel backward through time.

That matters because it moves retrocausality away from bad science-fiction mechanics and toward global constraint:

- probabilities may be assigned to whole histories or mosaics;
- laws may constrain entire spacetime patterns;
- local time-evolution may be a useful human-facing representation rather than the deepest form of law;
- what looks like backward causation from inside time may be an artefact of describing a whole-history constraint in temporal language.

This connects directly to the existing [QBism, Global Constraints, and Observer-Dependent Reality](qbism-adlam-observer-dependent-reality.md) page. Adlam is not making the QBist move. She is not saying quantum states are personal expectations. She is questioning whether laws must always be local forward-evolution rules.

## Wheeler Versus Adlam

| Question | Wheeler delayed choice | Adlam two roads |
|---|---|---|
| Main pressure point | Measurement context and what can be said about the past | Locality, nonlocality, relativity, and temporal explanation |
| Central example | Interferometer or cosmic delayed-choice setup | Bell-style correlations and relativistic ordering problems |
| Retrocausality role | Suggestive, rhetorical, participatory | Explicitly analyzed as possible law/explanation structure |
| Best safe reading | The photon's past is not a context-free classical route | Forward-only temporal locality may be an unjustified assumption |
| Main danger | Turning it into "mind changes past" | Turning it into "future signals travel backward" |
| Fit in this wiki | Wheeler/information/observer-participancy lane | Adlam/global-constraint/laws-as-whole-history lane |

Wheeler gives the intuition pump. Adlam gives the sorting mechanism.

Wheeler says: look how strange it is that a late experimental choice changes which past-description is available.

Adlam says: fine, now separate the options. Are you trying to rescue locality from Bell? Or are you accepting nonlocality and asking how it can be compatible with relativity without a preferred time order?

## Other Relevant Retrocausality Work

### Leifer And Pusey

Leifer and Pusey sharpen Huw Price's time-symmetry argument. They argue that, under assumptions including a form of time symmetry and lambda-mediation, a non-retrocausal time-symmetric ontology is not available for quantum theory.

This matters because it gives the retrocausality discussion a theorem-like backbone. It does not prove that retrocausality is true. It says: if you want a certain kind of time-symmetric ontology, you may have to accept retrocausal dependence.

### Huw Price

Price is a key philosophical ancestor of the modern retrocausality revival. His broader point is that physics may have inherited a time-asymmetric explanatory bias from our agent perspective rather than from the fundamental laws themselves.

That idea is very close to Adlam's complaint about temporal locality: we should not assume the universe computes the future from the past merely because that is how humans like to predict things.

### Wharton And Argaman

Wharton and Argaman defend "locally mediated" reformulations where correlations are mediated through spacetime-local parameters but some of those parameters depend on future measurement settings. They describe these models as partly solved all-at-once rather than as a story unfolding only from past to future.

This is one of the most relevant technical neighbours for Adlam's first road: retrocausality as a way to preserve local mediation.

### Aharonov, Bergmann, Lebowitz, And TSVF

The Aharonov-Bergmann-Lebowitz rule and the later two-state-vector formalism use both pre-selection and post-selection to describe quantum systems between measurements. This is time-symmetric and retrocausality-adjacent, but it should not automatically be treated as proof of physical backward causation.

Use TSVF here as a formal neighbour: it shows that quantum theory can be organized with both past and future boundary conditions in view.

### Cramer And Transactional Interpretation

Cramer's transactional interpretation uses offer and confirmation waves, inspired partly by Wheeler-Feynman absorber ideas, to describe quantum events as completed transactions. It is one of the classic explicitly retrocausal interpretations.

It belongs in the map, but with caveats. It is a developed interpretation, not a consensus replacement for standard quantum mechanics.

### Superdeterminism

Superdeterminism and retrocausality both relax measurement-setting independence, but they do it with different stories. Superdeterminism usually says hidden variables and future measurement settings are correlated because of past conditions. Retrocausality says future settings can be part of the explanation of earlier ontic states or whole-history constraints.

They are easy to confuse because both threaten the naive "free independent settings" assumption in Bell arguments. Keep them distinct unless a source explicitly links them.

## Charli's Working Interpretation

This is the useful non-ridiculous version:

Reality may be less like a film strip being generated one frame at a time, and more like a globally constrained pattern that local agents encounter from inside.

Wheeler helps because he attacks the idea that a quantum system always has a classical past itinerary waiting to be revealed.

Adlam helps because she attacks the deeper habit underneath that itinerary story: the assumption that proper physical explanation must always run from earlier local state to later local state.

The big move is not "the future changes the past." The big move is: maybe past and future are not related by a one-way production line at the most fundamental level. Maybe some explanations are whole-pattern constraints, and our forward-causal language is a local interface for agents like us.

That fits [Practical Agency Inside Constraint](../wiki/concepts/practical-agency-inside-constraint.md): global constraint does not erase agency. It changes what agency means. Instead of metaphysical escape from the pattern, agency is local model-building, intervention, and constraint-navigation from inside it.

## Do Not Overclaim

- Do not say Wheeler proves retrocausality.
- Do not say delayed choice proves the past is unreal.
- Do not say Adlam proves the future causes the past.
- Do not say retrocausality allows signalling to yesterday.
- Do not collapse retrocausality into superdeterminism.
- Do not treat all-at-once lawhood as simulation theory.
- Do not treat "observer" as necessarily meaning conscious human.

The conservative claim is strong enough: quantum foundations gives serious reasons to question simple forward-only, observer-independent, context-free descriptions of physical reality.
