# Bell Inequalities

Bell inequalities are constraints on correlations. They say: if measurement outcomes are governed by a locally causal hidden-variable story, then the correlations between distant measurements cannot exceed a certain bound.

Quantum mechanics predicts, and experiments observe, correlations that violate those bounds.

## The Core Logic

Bell's theorem is not simply "quantum mechanics is weird." It is a conditional argument:

1. Assume measurement outcomes can be explained by variables carried by the systems.
2. Assume choices and outcomes at one wing of the experiment do not depend on spacelike-separated choices and outcomes at the other wing.
3. Assume measurement settings are not secretly pre-correlated with the hidden variables.
4. Derive an inequality.
5. Observe that quantum predictions and experiments violate it.

The consequence is that at least one assumption in the package fails.

## Why Bell Matters For Wigner's Friend

Brukner and local-friendliness work borrow Bell's strategy. The target changes:

- Bell: can local hidden variables explain entangled correlations?
- Brukner: can all observers' facts be fitted into one observer-independent account while preserving locality and free choice?
- Local friendliness: can observed events be absolute while preserving local agency?

The family resemblance is the inequality form. A set of assumptions implies a statistical bound; quantum theory violates it.

## What Bell Does Not Prove By Itself

Bell violations do not by themselves choose between many-worlds, QBism, relational quantum mechanics, objective collapse, Bohmian mechanics, or superdeterminism. Each interpretation avoids the contradiction by rejecting or revising a different assumption.

## Experimental Status

Bell inequality violations are experimentally robust. Historic tests include Aspect, Dalibard, and Roger 1982, with later loophole-free tests strengthening the case. This wiki uses Bell primarily as the conceptual and mathematical ancestor of observer-independence and local-friendliness tests.
