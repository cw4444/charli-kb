# Glossary

## Absoluteness Of Observed Events

The assumption that if an event is observed by one observer, it is an event for all observers, not merely relative to the observer who registered it.

## Bell Inequality

A mathematical bound on correlations that follows from locality plus additional assumptions such as hidden variables or jointly defined outcomes. Quantum mechanics can violate Bell inequalities.

## Block Universe

A view of spacetime where past, present, and future are treated as parts of a single four-dimensional structure rather than as a world that only the present moment truly occupies. In this wiki, use carefully: block-universe-adjacent does not automatically mean fatalism, simulation theory, or observer-created reality.

## CHSH Inequality

The most common experimentally usable Bell inequality, introduced by Clauser, Horne, Shimony, and Holt.

## Consistent Histories

An interpretation where quantum descriptions must be carried out within a single consistent framework of histories. Different incompatible frameworks may be useful, but they cannot be freely combined into one account.

## Extended Wigner's Friend

A Wigner's-friend scenario expanded to multiple agents/labs so that consistency, locality, or Bell-style assumptions can be tested or formalized.

## Freedom Of Choice / Measurement Independence

The assumption that measurement settings are not secretly correlated with hidden variables relevant to the systems being measured.

## Local Agency

In local friendliness, the assumption that agents can freely choose interventions and that those choices are not correlated with events outside their future light cone.

## Local Friendliness

A no-go framework combining local agency with absoluteness of observed events. Quantum theory can violate inequalities derived from these assumptions.

## Locality

Roughly, the idea that events in one spacetime region are not influenced faster than light by choices or events in a spacelike-separated region. Exact meanings vary across Bell, relativity, and no-go theorem contexts.

## Global Constraint

A lawlike condition on an entire pattern or history, rather than a rule that only evolves an earlier state into a later state. Relevant to Adlam-style all-at-once lawhood and temporal-nonlocality questions.

## Heisenberg Cut

The boundary between what is treated quantum mechanically and what is treated classically in measurement descriptions. Everett is attractive partly because it does not require a fundamental cut.

## Many-Worlds / Everett

An interpretation that keeps universal unitary quantum evolution and denies that measurement selects one unique global outcome. Outcomes are stable within branches or relative states.

## Observer-Independent Fact

A fact treated as part of one shared observer-independent world, not merely as relative to a particular observer or measurement context.

## QBism

An interpretation where quantum states and probabilities express an agent's personal expectations, constrained by the Born rule.

## Participatory Universe

Wheeler's view that observation, registration, and information are not optional extras in physics but part of how physical reality is articulated.

## Temporal Nonlocality

The possibility that physical explanation may involve relations across time that are not reducible to ordinary forward-in-time causal propagation.

## Relational Quantum Mechanics

An interpretation where physical states and properties are relative to other physical systems rather than absolute.

## Superobserver

An outside observer, such as Wigner, who treats another observer and their lab as a quantum system.

## Universal Quantum Theory

The assumption that quantum theory applies to all physical systems, including observers, measuring devices, and laboratories.

## Wigner's Friend

A thought experiment in which a friend observes a quantum system inside a lab while Wigner describes the entire lab quantum mechanically from outside.
